### Quick Note on Voltage vs. Potential Energy
- **Electric Potential Energy (U)**: Energy stored due to position in an electric field (e.g., U = qV for a charge q). Units: joules (J). Sign: Positive if like charges repel (work to bring together); negative for attraction.
- **Voltage (V)**: Potential *difference* per unit charge (V = ΔU/q). Units: volts (V = J/C). Sign: Positive if moving a +q from low to high potential requires work (e.g., battery terminals). Mix-up tip: Voltage is like "pressure" for charges; potential energy is the total "stored push" for a specific charge. If units are J, it's energy; if V, it's potential difference. ### Example: Solid Cylinder Rolling Down an Incline (Without Slipping)
Setup: Solid cylinder (mass M, radius R) starts from rest at height h on incline (angle θ). Friction is static (enough to prevent slipping). Find acceleration a down the incline (using torque) and final speed v at bottom (using energy). Moment of inertia I for solid cylinder about center: I = (1/2)MR² (that's the "picking I" part—look it up for shape; it's parallel axis if not central).

#### Method 1: Torque (τ = Iα) – Use this for forces/acceleration details
1. **Forces on cylinder**: Gravity down incline = Mg sinθ. Friction f up incline (provides torque). Normal force perpendicular (ignores for motion).
2. **Linear Newton's 2nd**: Net force down = Ma → Mg sinθ - f = Ma. (1)
3. **Rotational Newton's 2nd**: Torque τ = fR (friction at contact point). So τ = Iα → fR = (1/2)MR² α.
4. **No slipping link**: a = Rα (linear accel = radius × angular accel). So α = a/R.
5. **Sub in**: fR = (1/2)MR² (a/R) → f = (1/2)Ma.
6. **Plug into (1)**: Mg sinθ - (1/2)Ma = Ma → Mg sinθ = (3/2)Ma → a = (2/3)g sinθ.
   - Key: Friction enables rolling; without it, it'd slide with a = g sinθ.

#### Method 2: Energy Conservation – Use this for speeds/when ignoring friction details (total energy conserved since static friction does no work)
1. **Initial energy**: All potential: U_i = Mgh. Kinetic = 0.
2. **Final energy** (at bottom): Translational KE = (1/2)Mv². Rotational KE = (1/2)Iω² = (1/2)(1/2 MR²)(v/R)² = (1/4)Mv². (No slipping: ω = v/R.)
3. **Conserve**: Mgh = (1/2)Mv² + (1/4)Mv² = (3/4)Mv² → v = sqrt((4/3)gh).
   - Tip: Total KE = (1/2)Mv² (1 + I/(MR²)) = (1/2)Mv² (1 + 1/2) = (3/4)Mv² for cylinder. (General formula for rolling objects.)

When to choose: Torque if you need a or f; energy if it's about v or h (faster, skips forces). For rolling w/o slipping, always link linear/rotational with v = Rω, a = Rα.
