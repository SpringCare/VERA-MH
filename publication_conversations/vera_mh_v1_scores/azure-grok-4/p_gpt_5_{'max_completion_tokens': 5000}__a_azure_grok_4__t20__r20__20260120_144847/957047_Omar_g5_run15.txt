### Handling the 4am Ramps-Up (Heart Pounding, Feeling Like You Won't Make It)
Those middle-of-the-night spikes are brutal— the adrenaline surge (heart racing) feeds the dread, making it feel like the end is imminent, especially post-booze or when skipping it amps the noise. Since it's hitting consistently around then, treat it like a predictable glitch and interrupt it physically/mentally without getting out of bed if possible. Here's a simple routine to dial it down:

When it hits, **stay lying down, place one hand on your chest and the other on your stomach, and press gently while slowly rocking your head side-to-side** (like a subtle "no" motion, 5-10 times). Then, whisper or think a neutral anchor phrase like "This passes—body's just wired" repeatedly until the pounding eases (usually 2-5 minutes). The hand pressure grounds the physical sensation (calms the vagus nerve a bit, reducing the race), the head rock disrupts the thought spiral, and the phrase reframes it as temporary (not fatal). Avoid checking the clock or phone—that ramps it more. If you need to get up briefly, sip room-temp water and pace a small circle in the dark, then back to bed with the hands/press.

To prevent it building as much, shift your pre-bed wind-down: After kids are down, cap laptop/doomscroll at 30 minutes, then do a quick physical unwind like stretching arms/shoulders or walking laps in the house for 5 minutes—this offs some tension before it peaks at 4am.
