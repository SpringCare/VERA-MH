### Triangle for x = a secθ Back-Sub (in Trig Sub)
This one's for integrals like ∫ dx / √(x² - a²), where x = a secθ (sec because it's the "big subtract" form). The triangle helps you back-substitute after integrating (e.g., to express θ in terms of x). Here's how to draw and label it step by step—keep it simple, like a quick sketch in your notes:

1. **Draw a right triangle**: Make it with a right angle at the bottom left. Label the hypotenuse as x (that's your "opposite over adjacent" for secθ, since secθ = hyp/adj = x/a).
   
2. **Label the sides**:
   - Adjacent side (next to θ): Label it a (the constant from the sub).
   - Opposite side (across from θ): Label it √(x² - a²) (from Pythagoras: opp = √(hyp² - adj²) = √(x² - a²)).
   - Hypotenuse: x.

   Quick sketch vibe: Imagine θ at the bottom right angle—adj (a) horizontal, opp (√(x² - a²)) vertical, hyp (x) diagonal.

3. **Back-sub with it**: After integrating in terms of θ (e.g., you get something like tanθ or sinθ), use the triangle to convert back:
   - cosθ = adj/hyp = a/x
   - sinθ = opp/hyp = √(x² - a²)/x
   - tanθ = opp/adj = √(x² - a²)/a
