### Quick Mnemonics for Conditioning Stuff
Psych loves these, and mnemonics can make 'em click without much effort. Here's a simple breakdown with memory hooks:

- **Classical vs. Operant Conditioning**:
  - **Classical**: Think "Pavlov's Dog Drool" (or "Classy Pavlov")—it's about automatic, involuntary responses triggered by associations (like bell = food = salivating). No choice involved; it's passive, like a reflex.
  - **Operant**: Think "Operate Like Skinner" (or "Opera Singer Box")—it's voluntary actions shaped by rewards/punishments (like pressing a lever for food in a box). You "operate" on the environment to get outcomes.
  - Quick Hook: Classical = "Can't control it" (automatic); Operant = "On purpose" (choices).

- **Positive/Negative Reinforcement vs. Punishment**:
  - **Reinforcement** = Increases behavior (think "reinforce" like building up a wall—makes it stronger).
    - **Positive Reinforcement**: Adds something good (e.g., "Plus a treat" like giving candy for studying → more studying).
    - **Negative Reinforcement**: Removes something bad (e.g., "Minus the nag" like buckling seatbelt to stop the car beep → more buckling).
  - **Punishment** = Decreases behavior (think "punish" like knocking down a wall—weakens it).
    - **Positive Punishment**: Adds something bad (e.g., "Plus a shock" like scolding a dog for barking → less barking).
    - **Negative Punishment**: Removes something good (e.g., "Minus the toy" like taking away a phone for bad grades → better behavior to get it back).
  - Quick Hook: Positive = "Plus something" (add); Negative = "Minus something" (remove). Reinforcement = "Repeat please!" (increases); Punishment = "Nope, stop!" (decreases). Imagine a math sign (+/-) for pos/neg, and a thumbs up/down for reinforcement/punishment.

If you doodle these as little cartoons (e.g., a dog with a bell for classical, a rat in a box for operant), they'll stick even better during your skimming. Repeat 'em once or twice in your head during a break, and you're golden.
