### Difference Between Intrusive Thoughts and Postpartum Psychosis
It's smart to want clarity on this so you can spot red flags—knowledge reduces fear. Intrusive thoughts (like yours) are common in postpartum anxiety/OCD and are usually not dangerous, while postpartum psychosis is a rare, serious emergency (affects about 1-2 in 1,000 births) involving a break from reality. Here's a clear breakdown:

- **Intrusive Thoughts (What You're Describing):**
  - Unwanted, ego-dystonic (feel alien, scary, and against your values—like "This freaks me out, I don't want this").
  - Vivid but fleeting "flashes" or "what ifs" (e.g., not waking up, dropping the baby) without delusions or loss of reality.
  - You recognize them as irrational/intrusive, feel distressed by them, and have no plan/intent to act.
  - Often triggered by stress, exhaustion, or hormones; they come and go, and you stay connected to reality.
  - No hallucinations (seeing/hearing things that aren't there) or fixed false beliefs.

- **Postpartum Psychosis (Rare and Different):**
  - Involves a loss of touch with reality, like delusions (fixed false beliefs, e.g., believing the baby is possessed or you're being persecuted) or hallucinations (hearing voices commanding harm).
  - Thoughts feel real and compelling, not intrusive/scary—you might believe them and act on them without recognizing they're irrational.
  - Rapid onset (often within 2 weeks postpartum), with extreme mood swings, confusion, paranoia, or disorganized thinking/behavior (e.g., talking nonsensically, severe insomnia, or hyperactivity).
  - Ego-syntonic (feels like part of you, not alien); there's often no insight that something's wrong.

- **Red Flags for Psychosis (Seek Immediate Help If Any Appear):**
  - Hallucinations or delusions as above.
  - Sudden severe confusion, disorientation, or bizarre behavior (e.g., not recognizing family).
  - Extreme agitation, mania, or rapid mood shifts that feel out of control.
  - Feeling commanded by voices or beliefs to harm yourself/baby (not just fleeting thoughts).
  - If this happens, it's a medical emergency—call 911 or go to the ER right away; it's treatable with hospitalization and meds.

From what you've shared, your experiences sound firmly in the intrusive thoughts camp (unwanted, scary, no reality break), not psychosis. But if anything shifts, use those 3am text options like 988 or TLC-MAMA.
