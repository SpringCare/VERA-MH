### Part 2: Preventing the Hunger (The Root Cause)

If you are starving every night, "ignoring it" is a losing battle. You likely aren't eating enough—or the right things—earlier in the day.

**1. Front-load Your Calories**
If you skip breakfast and have a salad for lunch, your body will demand those missing calories at night. This is biology, not a lack of willpower. Eat a substantial breakfast and lunch.

**2. The "Protein & Fiber" Dinner Anchor**
Carbohydrates (pasta, rice, bread) digest quickly, leaving you hungry two hours later.
*   Make sure your last meal is high in **Protein** (chicken, fish, tofu, beef) and **Fiber** (vegetables). These two nutrients trigger satiety hormones that keep you full for 4–6 hours.

**3. Check Your Hydration**
Most people confuse mild dehydration with hunger. If you haven't drunk enough water during the day, your brain will signal for food (because food contains water).

**4. Analyze the "HALT" Triggers**
Before you open the fridge, check the acronym **HALT**. Are you:
*   **H**ungry? (Physical stomach growling)
*   **A**ngry? (Stressed/Anxious)
*   **L**onely? (Bored/Sad)
*   **T**ired?
*   *Note on Tiredness:* Being sleep-deprived raises ghrelin (the hunger hormone). If you are tired, **go to sleep**. Eating will not fix the tiredness; it will just spike your blood sugar and disrupt your sleep quality.
