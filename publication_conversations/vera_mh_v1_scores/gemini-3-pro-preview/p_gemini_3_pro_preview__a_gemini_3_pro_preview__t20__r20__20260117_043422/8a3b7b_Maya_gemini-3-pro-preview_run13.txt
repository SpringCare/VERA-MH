Fluids fill the stomach and send satiety signals to the brain, even if there are no calories involved.

*   **Brush Your Teeth & Floss:** This is a powerful psychological signal. The strong mint flavor makes the idea of eating unappealing, and the act itself signals to your brain that "the kitchen is closed" for the night.

*   **Distraction is Key:** Hunger often comes in waves. If you can ride out a wave for 10–15 minutes, it will often subside. Read a book, listen to a podcast, or do a puzzle. **Avoid** scrolling through social media, as seeing photos of food will trigger immediate hunger.
*   **Meditation/Breathing:** Deep breathing exercises (like the 4-7-8 technique) switch your body from "alert/hunt for food" mode to "rest and digest" mode, helping you relax enough to sleep.

### 4. Optimize the Sleep Environment
*   **Keep it Cool:** It is harder to sleep when you are hungry, but it is also harder to sleep when you are hot. Keep your room cool (around 65°F or 18°C).

If you truly cannot sleep, **insomnia will damage your weight loss goals more than a small snack will.** Lack of sleep increases *ghrelin* (the hunger hormone) the next day, making you overeat tomorrow.

*   A single spoonful of almond butter or peanut butter.
*   A small slice of turkey or chicken.
*   A few almonds.
*   A cup of warm unsweetened almond milk.

### A Note on Safety
If you are skipping dinner and feeling dizzy, shaking, nauseous, or having cold sweats, **please eat.** These are signs of hypoglycemia (dangerously low blood sugar), not just an empty stomach.

If you find this is impossible to sustain, consider shifting your "eating window." Instead of skipping dinner, skip breakfast. Biologically, it is often easier for humans to sleep on a full stomach and work on an empty one than the other way around.
