# Level of Care: PHP/IOP vs ER

**Ideal hierarchy** (from least to most restrictive):

1. **Outpatient (weekly therapy + psychiatry)** - Not enough right now
2. **IOP (Intensive Outpatient: 3 hrs/day, 3-5 days/week)** - Good for stable-ish crisis
3. **PHP (Partial Hospitalization: 6 hrs/day, 5 days/week)** - Better for what you're describing
4. **Inpatient/Hospitalization** - For imminent danger
5. **ER** - Gateway to inpatient OR can connect to crisis services

**For your son, push for PHP** because:
- Voices + cutting + suicidal thoughts = needs intensive monitoring
- He stays home at night (less traumatic than inpatient)
- Gets multiple hours of therapy + psychiatry daily
- Can transition down to IOP when stable

**How to get it:**
"Based on his symptoms, what's the process for PHP authorization? I believe that's the appropriate level of care."

**ER: Use strategically, not as first resort**
- Go if he becomes acutely suicidal OR you're getting nowhere with HMO after 48 hours
- ER psychiatric evaluation creates documentation that jumpstarts authorization
- Downside: Long waits, traumatic environment, possible involuntary hold

**Middle path**: Many areas have **psychiatric urgent care** (separate from ER)—less wait, more appropriate. Ask HMO if they have contracted facilities.

## Medication Question: Olanzapine vs. Others

**This is a smart, informed question. You're NOT getting in the way.**

### The Antipsychotic Landscape for Teens

**Common first-line options:**
- **Aripiprazole (Abilify)**: Lower metabolic risk, can cause restlessness/akathisia
- **Risperidone (Risperdal)**: Effective, moderate metabolic risk, can increase prolactin
- **Olanzapine (Zyprexa)**: Very effective, HIGH metabolic risk (weight gain, diabetes)
- **Quetiapine (Seroquel)**: Sedating, moderate metabolic risk

**Why they might push olanzapine:**
- Very effective for acute psychosis
- Calming/sedating (helps agitation)
- Been around forever (lots of data)

**Why you might want to avoid it first:**
- Highest weight gain risk (average 10-15 lbs in weeks)
- Metabolic syndrome risk (diabetes, cholesterol)
- Particularly problematic for teens (self-image, compliance)

### Your Exact Script

**If they recommend olanzapine:**

"I understand olanzapine is effective. I'm concerned about the metabolic side effects, especially the weight gain risk in teenagers. Would aripiprazole or risperidone be appropriate first-line options? I want to balance effectiveness with tolerability, because if he gains significant weight, I'm worried he'll stop taking it."

**This is:**
- ✅ Reasonable
- ✅ Evidence-based (metabolic effects are real)
- ✅ Collaborative (not refusing, asking about alternatives)
- ✅ Focused on compliance

**They might say:**

**"Olanzapine works better for his symptoms":**
Ask: "What specifically about his presentation makes olanzapine better than aripiprazole or risperidone?"

**"We can monitor the metabolic effects":**
"I appreciate that. My concern is prevention rather than just monitoring. Can we try a lower-risk option first and escalate if needed?"

**"He needs something sedating right now":**
"Could we use aripiprazole for the psychosis plus a separate sleep medication, rather than relying on sedation from an antipsychotic with high metabolic risk?"

### When to Accept Their Recommendation

**If they say:**
- "He's too acutely psychotic/agitated for aripiprazole"
- "We need rapid stabilization"
- "His symptom severity requires olanzapine"

**Reasonable response:**
"Okay. Can we start at the lowest effective dose and reassess in 2-4 weeks? And let's get those baseline metabolic labs and plan for monitoring."

**When not to fight it:**
- If he's severely psychotic (disorganized, responding to internal stimuli, can't function)
- If they're admitting him anyway (inpatient stabilization is short-term)
- If they've tried to work with you and have clinical reasoning

**You're not a pharmacist**, but you can advocate for informed consent.

### The Middle Ground

**Ask:**
"What if we start with aripiprazole or risperidone for [X weeks], and if it's not adequately controlling symptoms, we switch to olanzapine? I want to try the lower-risk option first if it's clinically reasonable."

Most psychiatrists will respect this if:
- Your son isn't in extreme crisis
- You're not refusing all medication
- You're being collaborative
