# Mobile crisis team - YES, try it:

**Mobile crisis is often BETTER than ER:**

**Pros:**
- They come to you (less traumatic)
- Can evaluate in home environment
- Can connect to outpatient services faster
- Avoid ER wait/chaos/cost
- They're mental health specialists

**Cons:**
- Not available everywhere
- May take hours to arrive
- Can't prescribe meds on the spot (though can arrange)

**Worth calling:** Google "[your county] mobile crisis team" or ask pediatrician for the number.

**They can:**
- Assess suicide risk professionally
- Create safety plan
- Fast-track to psychiatrist
- Sometimes do short-term home visits

**Not "too much"** - this is exactly what they're for.

# Mobile crisis vs ER timing:

**Call mobile crisis FIRST thing in the morning** (like 8-9am):
- Ask their estimated arrival time
- If it's 4-6 hours AND he's stable, you can wait
- If he's deteriorating, go to ER instead
- Mobile crisis can also advise "go to ER now" if needed

**Go straight to ER if:**
- He wakes up in crisis mode
- Voices are worse/commanding
- He's agitated or won't stay near you
- Your gut says don't wait

**Stable morning = wait for mobile crisis.** They're better equipped than ER for ongoing care.

# Antipsychotic medication + labs:

**If pediatrician wants to start antipsychotic:**

**Ideal:** Labs/ECG first, especially:
- Baseline metabolic panel
- ECG (some antipsychotics affect heart rhythm)
- Weight/vitals

**Reality:** If there's a wait for labs, many providers start low-dose and do labs within first week.

**Your question to ask:** "What are we starting, what are the risks, when do we do labs, and what do I watch for?"

**Common first choices:** Risperidone, aripiprazole (Abilify) - relatively safe, fast-acting.

**You can ask:** "Can we do labs today or tomorrow before starting?" Most pediatricians can order stat labs.

**Don't refuse medication while waiting for perfect lab timing** - voices + self-harm = medication may be urgent.

# Voices saying "hurt yourself" but he says he won't:

**This is the hardest call:**

**Probably ER if:**
- Voices are COMMANDING (not just suggesting)
- They're constant/loud
- He seems like he's struggling to resist
- He's less certain about resisting as time goes on

**Possibly manageable at home if:**
- He's adamant he won't listen to them
- He's telling you about them (transparency = good)
- Mobile crisis is coming soon
- He stays near you and engaged

**Call mobile crisis or 988 to help assess this** - describe exactly what he's saying.

**When in doubt: ER.** You can't un-do a tragedy.
