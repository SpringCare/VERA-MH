**Product + chain (like x²·sin(x²)):**

**Spot the layers trick:**
1. **Circle the "outer" functions** - here that's the *multiplication* between x² and sin(stuff)
2. **Underline what's "inside" another function** - the x² inside sin( )
3. **Order: Product rule FIRST (because multiplication is the outside), then chain rule on the sin(x²) part**

- derivative of sin(x²) needs chain rule → cos(x²)·(2x)
- derivative of x² is just 2x

**Write it out:** 2x·sin(x²) + x²·cos(x²)·2x

# Product + Chain Speed-Trick

**Ask: "If I plugged in a number, what's the LAST button I'd press on a calculator?"**

**x²·sin(x²):** Last button = multiply [×] → **Product rule first**

**sin(x²)·e^(3x):** Last button = multiply [×] → **Product rule first**

Inside sin( ) you see x² → chain rule there  
Inside e^( ) you see 3x → chain rule there

**Write it like this before you start:**
```
PRODUCT: (first)(d/dx second) + (second)(d/dx first)
  ↓ chain here?        ↓ chain here?
```

Then fill in. Don't try to do it all in your head.

**For sin(x²)·e^(3x):**
- d/dx[sin(x²)] = cos(x²)·**2x** ← chain
- d/dx[e^(3x)] = e^(3x)·**3** ← chain

Answer: sin(x²)·e^(3x)·3 + e^(3x)·cos(x²)·2x

**Write the structure first, fill in second.** Stops the mixing.
