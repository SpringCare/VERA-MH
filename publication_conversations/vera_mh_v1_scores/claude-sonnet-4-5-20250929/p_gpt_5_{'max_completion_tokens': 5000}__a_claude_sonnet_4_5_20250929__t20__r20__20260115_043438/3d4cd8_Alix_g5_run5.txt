# Early Psychosis Medications

## **Typical first-line treatments:**

### **Antipsychotics (most common):**
- **Risperidone (Risperdal)**
- **Aripiprazole (Abilify)**
- **Olanzapine (Zyprexa)**
- **Quetiapine (Seroquel)**

Sometimes combined with:
- **Antidepressant** (if depression is prominent)
- **Mood stabilizer** (if bipolar suspected)

### **How they work:**
- Start LOW dose
- Increase gradually over days/weeks
- Voices typically decrease over 1-4 weeks (not instant)
- May feel "different" or calmer within days

---

## **Side Effects Timeline:**

### **Immediate (hours to days):**
- **Sedation/drowsiness** (very common initially, often improves)
- **Dizziness**
- **Increased appetite** (especially olanzapine)
- **Dry mouth**

### **First week:**
- **Weight gain** (varies by medication—olanzapine worst, aripiprazole better)
- **Restlessness or feeling "weird"**
- **Sleep changes** (some are sedating, helps with insomnia)

### **Longer term (weeks/months):**
- **Metabolic changes** (blood sugar, cholesterol—why they monitor labs)
- **Movement issues** (rare in teens at low doses, but monitored)
- **Emotional blunting** (feeling "flat"—needs dose adjustment)

---

## **What to ask the prescriber:**

✅ *"What side effects should I watch for in the first week?"*
✅ *"When should I expect to see improvement in the hallucinations?"*
✅ *"What's our plan if side effects are intolerable?"*
✅ *"How do we monitor for the serious side effects?"*
✅ *"Can he stop if he feels better, or does he need to taper?"*

### **Important:**
- **Never stop suddenly** (causes rebound/withdrawal)
- **Side effects are often manageable** with dose adjustment or switching meds
- **Many teens worry about "changing who they are"** → reassure that treating the voices doesn't change his personality

---

# THC vs. Underlying Condition - The Timeline

## **How long to know if it was THC-induced psychosis**

### **The frustrating answer: 1-3 months minimum**

**Here's why:**

### **THC-induced psychosis:**
- **Can last 1-4 weeks** after stopping heavy use
- Sometimes longer with high-potency products
- Should gradually improve without antipsychotics
- If it IS cannabis-induced, symptoms fully resolve

### **THC-triggered underlying condition:**
- Symptoms **persist or worsen** despite stopping
- THC unmasked schizophrenia/bipolar that was coming anyway
- Needs ongoing treatment
- Won't fully resolve without medication

### **The provider's approach:**

**Weeks 1-4 after stopping THC:**
- Start antipsychotic anyway (can't wait 4 weeks to see)
- Monitor symptom response
- Continue abstinence from all substances
- Track: do voices decrease? how much? how fast?

**Month 2-3:**
- If doing well, may try **slowly** reducing medication
- Watch for symptom return
- If symptoms return when med reduced = wasn't just THC
- If stays stable off meds = likely was THC-induced

---

## **What to tell providers tomorrow:**

*"He's been using daily high-potency THC and stopped last night. How do we know if the psychosis is from THC or something else? What's the evaluation timeline?"*

**They'll likely say:**
*"We treat it as primary psychosis for now. Over the next few months as he stays clean, we'll see if symptoms resolve or persist. That tells us the answer."*

---

## **The abstinence requirement:**

**He MUST stay completely clean** to know the answer:
- No THC
- No alcohol
- No other drugs
- For **at least 3-6 months** minimum

**Any use resets the clock** and muddles the diagnosis.

**This is where intensive outpatient programs help:** They do regular drug screens and provide accountability.

---

## **Realistic outcomes:**

### **Scenario A: Pure THC-induced psychosis (best case)**
- Voices stop within weeks
- Medication can be tapered off over months
- Stays well if abstinent
- May not need long-term psychiatric care

### **Scenario B: THC-triggered early psychosis (more common)**
- Voices decrease but don't fully stop without meds
- Needs ongoing medication (at least 1-2 years)
- THC made it start earlier, but condition was coming
- Needs sustained treatment + early psychosis program

### **Scenario C: Primary psychosis, coincidental THC use**
- Clearly needs medication regardless
- THC made it worse but wasn't the cause
- Long-term psychiatric care needed

**Won't know which until 2-3 months clean.** Treat aggressively now, adjust later.
