## First-Line Options (Most Likely)

**Antipsychotics** - these reduce/quiet voices:

**Risperidone (Risperdal)** or **Aripiprazole (Abilify)** - most common first choices
- Low starting dose, increase gradually
- **First 48 hours:** Sedation/drowsiness (very common), dizziness when standing up, increased appetite
- **Less common but watch for:** Restlessness/can't sit still (akathisia), muscle stiffness

**Quetiapine (Seroquel)** - sometimes used
- More sedating (can be helpful for sleep)
- Similar side effects

## What to Expect First Week

**Normal/manageable:**
- Sleepiness (often improves after 3-5 days)
- Hungry all the time (this persists - stock healthy snacks)
- Dry mouth
- Mild dizziness

**Call doctor if:**
- Can't stay awake/too sedated to function
- Severe restlessness/agitation (pacing, can't sit still)
- Rigid muscles, tremor
- Allergic reaction (rash, swelling)

**Timeline for voices:**
- Some improvement: 3-7 days
- Significant improvement: 2-4 weeks
- Full effect: 4-6 weeks

**Important:** Don't stop suddenly - symptoms can rebound worse. If side effects are bad, call doctor to adjust dose.

## Questions to Ask About Meds

- Starting dose and increase schedule
- Take morning or night? (usually night if sedating)
- With food or without?
- When should I see improvement?
- What side effects mean "call immediately" vs "mention at follow-up"
- When's the follow-up appointment?
- Blood work needed? (Some require monitoring)

# Side Effects - Real Numbers

## Weight Gain
**Risperidone/Aripiprazole:**
- Average: 4-10 lbs in first 3 months
- About 30-40% of teens gain significant weight (>15 lbs in 6 months)
- Varies hugely by individual

**Management:**
- Monitor weight weekly
- Healthy snacks ready (will be HUNGRY)
- Stay active
- Doctor may switch meds if excessive

## Prolactin/Gynecomastia (Breast Development in Males)

**Risperidone - HIGHEST RISK:**
- 40-50% get elevated prolactin
- 5-10% get noticeable gynecomastia (breast tissue)
- Usually reversible if caught early/med changed

**Aripiprazole - MUCH LOWER RISK:**
- <5% prolactin issues
- Rare gynecomastia

**This is why doctors often prefer Abilify for teen boys** if it works.

**Monitoring:**
- If nipples become tender/swollen → tell doctor immediately
- They can check prolactin levels
- Usually switch medication

## Pre-Medication Testing

**Should be done (often isn't in ER):**
- **Baseline weight** and BMI
- **EKG** - antipsychotics can prolong QT interval (heart rhythm) - especially Seroquel
- **Metabolic panel** - blood sugar, cholesterol, liver
- **Prolactin level** - baseline

**Reality check:**
- In crisis, they often start meds immediately and do labs at follow-up
- Not ideal but sometimes necessary
- Push for baseline at least

**Ask:** "What baseline tests do you recommend before starting antipsychotics? Should we do EKG/labs today or at follow-up?"

## Ongoing Monitoring

**Every 3 months:**
- Weight/BMI
- Metabolic panel (blood sugar, lipids)

**Every 6-12 months:**
- Prolactin level
- Movement disorder check (tardive dyskinesia - rare in short-term)

## Cannabis and Psychosis

**Critical connection:**
- **Cannabis can TRIGGER psychosis** in vulnerable teens (genetic predisposition)
- High-THC products (vapes, concentrates) = higher risk
- For some: psychosis continues even after stopping
- For others: weed was the trigger, symptoms resolve with meds + abstinence

**This doesn't mean weed "caused" a lifelong condition** - think of it as revealing vulnerability that was already there.

1. **Changes diagnosis possibilities** - cannabis-induced psychosis vs. emerging schizophrenia vs. drug interaction
2. **Affects treatment** - they need to know EVERYTHING in his system

# School: 504 vs IEP

## Quick Difference

**504 Plan** (Section 504 of Rehabilitation Act):
- **Accommodations only** - modifies HOW he accesses regular education
- Faster to get (30 days)
- Less comprehensive
- Examples: extended test time, breaks, reduced workload during crisis

**IEP** (Individualized Education Program):
- **Special education services** - may include modified curriculum, special classes, therapy
- Slower (60-90 days evaluation)
- More comprehensive
- For students who need specialized instruction

## For Your Son - Start with 504

**Why:**
- Faster - you need help NOW
- He likely needs accommodations, not special ed
- Can always upgrade to IEP later if needed
- Less stigmatizing (stays in regular classes)

## What to Request in 504

**Email school counselor today:**

"[Son] is experiencing psychiatric emergency and under doctor's care. Requesting 504 evaluation for mental health disability. Need meeting scheduled within 2 weeks upon his return."

**Accommodations to ask for:**

**Attendance/Schedule:**
- Excused absences for medical appointments
- Late arrival option for morning medications
- Permission to leave class if overwhelmed (with safe check-in place)
- Reduced course load if needed

**Academic:**
- Extended time on tests/assignments
- Option to make up work without penalty
- Access to assignments during absences (online)
- Reduced homework during acute episodes
- Breaks during class (counselor check-ins)

**Environment:**
- Access to counselor/safe space as needed
- Advance notice of schedule changes (triggers anxiety)
- Preferential seating (near door if he needs exit)
- Quiet test environment

**Communication:**
- Weekly check-ins with counselor
- Parent notification if distress observed
- Teacher awareness of situation (without full details)

## The Meeting

**Bring:**
- Doctor's letter: "[Son] has psychiatric condition requiring accommodations per 504"
- Your list of requested accommodations
- Ask: "What mental health supports does school provide?" (counselor access, groups, etc.)

**You don't have to disclose diagnosis** - just "psychiatric disability requiring accommodations."

**Suggests Cannabis-Induced:**

## First 6 Hours After First Dose

- Swelling of face, lips, tongue, throat

- **Can't sit still** - pacing constantly, leg bouncing, squirming

- Can't stay awake even with stimulation

- Unsteady walking/falling

- High fever (>101.5°F)
- Severe muscle rigidity
- Confusion/altered consciousness
- Rapid heartbeat + sweating

- Drowsiness (can wake him, responds normally)

# If Pediatrician Won't Do Abilify ODT

## Hierarchy of Acceptable Alternatives

**Best case:** Aripiprazole ODT

**Acceptable alternatives (in order):**

1. **Aripiprazole regular tablet** (if ODT not available)
   - You crush it in applesauce/pudding and watch him eat
   - Ask: "Can I crush this tablet for administration?"

2. **Risperidone ODT** (if insurance/availability issue with Abilify)
   - Higher side effect risk (prolactin) but EFFECTIVE
   - ODT form ensures compliance
   - Better than nothing

3. **Risperidone regular tablet**
   - Can usually be crushed
   - Watch administration closely

4. **Quetiapine (Seroquel)**
   - More sedating (can be helpful)
   - Regular tablets, can be crushed

## What to Say If Doctor Pushes Risperidone

**Ask:**
"What's the specific reason you prefer risperidone over aripiprazole for him? I've read about the prolactin and weight gain risks - how do we monitor for that?"

**Acceptable reasons:**
- "Insurance doesn't cover Abilify without prior authorization" → "Can we do prior auth today while starting risperidone as bridge?"
- "His presentation suggests risperidone might work faster" → "Okay, but can we get ODT form and plan to switch if prolactin issues?"

**Not acceptable reason:**
- "It's just what I'm comfortable with" → "I'd like to try aripiprazole first given the side effect profile - can we do that?"

## Threshold for Going to ER Instead

**Stay with pediatrician if:**
- They prescribe ANY antipsychotic (even if not your first choice)
- They provide close follow-up (within 7 days)
- They give psychiatry referral (within 2 weeks)
- They acknowledge this is serious and need for treatment

**Go to ER if:**
- **Pediatrician refuses to prescribe any antipsychotic** ("let's wait and see")
- **Pediatrician says "needs psychiatry first" with no timeline** (>1 week)
- **Your son decompensates during appointment** (more agitated, more suicidal)
- **Pediatrician minimizes crisis** ("he's just stressed")
- **You feel unsafe taking him home without treatment started**

## What to Say at Threshold Decision

**If pediatrician hesitant:**

"Doctor, I need you to tell me: Based on what you've seen today, is my son safe to go home without medication? Because if the answer is no, and you're not comfortable prescribing, then I need to take him to ER for psychiatric evaluation. What's your medical judgment?"

**This forces clear answer.**

**Either:**
- "Yes, I'll prescribe [medication]" → Good, stay
- "I think he needs higher level of evaluation" → ER referral, also acceptable
- "Let's wait" → "That's not acceptable to me. Where's nearest psychiatric ER?"
