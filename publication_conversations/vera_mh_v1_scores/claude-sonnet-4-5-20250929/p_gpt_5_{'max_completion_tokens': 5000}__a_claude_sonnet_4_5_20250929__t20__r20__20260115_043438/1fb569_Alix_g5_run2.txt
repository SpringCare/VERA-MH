# MEDICATIONS - Labs & Monitoring

## If They Start Antipsychotic (Risperidone/Olanzapine/Abilify)

### BEFORE First Dose - Should Check:

**Standard labs:**
- ✅ **Metabolic panel** (glucose, liver, kidney) - already doing for medical clearance
- ✅ **CBC** - baseline
- ✅ **Lipid panel** (cholesterol/triglycerides) - these meds can affect metabolism
- ✅ **EKG** - check QTc interval (some antipsychotics can prolong it - heart risk)
- ✅ **Weight, height, BMI** - baseline (these meds cause weight gain)
- ✅ **Blood pressure** - baseline

**Less common but good practice:**
- Prolactin level (antipsychotics can elevate it)
- Hemoglobin A1c (diabetes risk)

**If they DON'T do these - ask why.** Some ERs do bare minimum and expect outpatient follow-up.

### Side Effects to Watch - First 2-3 Days:

**COMMON (tell them, not concerning):**
- **Sedation/drowsiness** - very common, often improves
- **Dizziness** when standing (orthostatic hypotension)
- **Dry mouth**
- **Increased appetite** (starts immediately with olanzapine especially)
- **Mild restlessness**

**CONCERNING (call doctor):**
- **Severe sedation** - can't wake him, slurred speech
- **Rapid heartbeat** or chest pain
- **Severe dizziness/fainting**
- **Difficulty urinating**
- **Fever** (rare but serious - could be neuroleptic malignant syndrome)

**SERIOUS (ER/911):**
- **Dystonic reaction** - muscle spasms, twisted neck, tongue protrusion, eyes rolling back (treatable, looks scary, happens within hours to days)
- **Can't stay still/severe agitation** (akathisia - severe form)
- **Seizure**
- **Allergic reaction** - rash, swelling, trouble breathing

### Specific Medication Notes:

**Risperidone (Risperdal):**
- Most likely: drowsiness, increased appetite, prolactin elevation (can cause breast tenderness/milk production even in males)
- Watch for: movement side effects

**Olanzapine (Zyprexa):**
- Most likely: **significant sedation, major appetite increase/weight gain**
- Watch for: metabolic changes (glucose, cholesterol)
- Often given at night due to sedation

**Aripiprazole (Abilify):**
- Most likely: restlessness (akathisia), headache, nausea
- Generally less sedating than others
- Less weight gain than olanzapine/risperidone

### What to Do:

**First dose:**
- Give with food
- Give at night if sedating
- Watch for 2-3 hours after first dose

**First 3 days:**
- Monitor for severe side effects above
- Track: sleep, appetite, mood, activity level
- Don't expect symptom improvement yet (takes 1-2 weeks)
- **Don't stop suddenly** - call prescriber with concerns

**Get from ER:**
- ✅ Written instructions about the medication
- ✅ What side effects to watch for
- ✅ When to call/return
- ✅ Follow-up plan for medication management

# 2. MEDICATION CONSENT - Teen Refusal Process

## The Legal Reality in CA:

**You CAN authorize medication even if he refuses** when:
- ✅ Emergency situation (which this is)
- ✅ You're the parent/legal guardian
- ✅ Provider recommends it

**BUT - the practical reality is more complex.**

## How ERs Actually Handle Teen Refusal:

### Typical Process:

**Step 1: Provider recommends medication**
- Explains to both of you: why, what it does, side effects
- Asks for his consent

**Step 2: If he refuses:**
- They talk to him alone: "What are your concerns?"
- Common fears: "Will this change who I am? Make me a zombie? Mean I'm crazy?"
- They address concerns

**Step 3: They talk to you:**
- "He's refusing. Here are the options:"

### Three Paths Forward:

**Option A: Voluntary with buy-in (BEST)**
- They negotiate with him
- You reinforce: "This is to help the whispers stop"
- He agrees, even reluctantly
- **Most common outcome**

**Option B: You authorize, they administer over objection**
- Requires clinical determination that it's medically necessary
- Usually only if:
  - He's being admitted anyway, OR
  - He's acutely unsafe and medication is immediate intervention
- **How it happens:**
  - IM (intramuscular) injection if he won't take oral
  - Usually in context of involuntary hold
- **Rare in ER for discharge home**

**Option C: They discharge without medication**
- Give you prescription to fill
- Recommend you work on getting him to agree
- Follow up with outpatient psychiatrist ASAP
- **Common when he's going home, not admitted**

## What You Can Say to Advocate:

**To the provider (privately if possible):**
"I'm his parent and I consent to medication you're recommending. I understand he's hesitant. What's the safest way forward? Can we discharge him on medication, or do you need him to agree first?"

**To your son (your words, this tone):**
"I know you're scared. I am too. The doctor thinks this medication can help stop the whispers and make you feel safer. We can try it and see. If side effects are bad, we'll talk to the doctor. But right now, I need you to trust me and try this. I'm making this decision because I love you and this is what's going to help."

## Realistic Outcome:

**If going home from ER:**
- Most providers won't force medication on a refusing teen who's going to outpatient care
- They'll give YOU prescription
- Expect you to convince him or get outpatient psychiatrist to work with him

**If being admitted:**
- They can medicate over objection if clinically necessary
- Court order not needed in emergency

## Your Legal Authority:

✅ You can pick up prescription even if he objects  
✅ You can authorize treatment  
✅ You cannot physically force pills down his throat at home  

**Practical reality:** If he strongly refuses at home, you're back to ER or calling mobile crisis.

## Best Approach:

**Frame it as:** 
- "We're trying this together"
- "This is to help you feel better"
- "We can adjust if needed"
- Not punishment, not "you're crazy" - it's medicine for symptoms

**Most teens eventually agree when:**
- Symptoms are distressing (he wants relief)
- They trust the parent/provider
- Side effects are managed
- They understand it's time-limited trial

## Who Manages Meds in the Gap?

**This is the critical question.** Here's the reality:

### Option 1: ER Bridge Prescription (COMMON)
- ER gives 5-7 day supply

- **Con:** No follow-up monitoring

- Not sustainable but sometimes necessary
