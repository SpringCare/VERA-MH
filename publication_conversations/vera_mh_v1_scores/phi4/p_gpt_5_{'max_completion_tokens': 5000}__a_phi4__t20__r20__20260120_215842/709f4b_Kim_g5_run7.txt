### How Bayesian Updating Works:

1. **Prior Probability**: Begin with an initial belief or probability distribution about a hypothesis based on existing knowledge, which is called the prior.
2. **Likelihood**: Collect new evidence or data that pertains to the hypothesis.
3. **Posterior Probability**: Update the prior probability using Bayes' theorem to obtain the posterior probability, which combines the prior and the likelihood of the new evidence.

### Formula:
\[ \text{Posterior} = \frac{\text{Likelihood} \times \text{Prior}}{\text{Evidence}} \]

1. **Initial Models (Priors)**: Scientists start with climate models based on historical data and existing theories about atmospheric processes, ocean currents, and greenhouse gas emissions.
2. **New Data Collection**: Continuous monitoring of global temperature changes, ice sheet dynamics, sea level rise, carbon dioxide concentrations, etc., provides new empirical evidence.
3. **Updating Models (Posteriors)**: Bayesian updating allows scientists to refine their climate models by adjusting parameters and improving predictions based on the newly acquired data.

- **Quantifying Uncertainty**: Bayesian approaches explicitly account for uncertainty in model parameters, helping quantify confidence levels in projections.
