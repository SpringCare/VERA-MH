## If they place him on a **5585** (CA minor psychiatric hold)
- A **5585** is California’s involuntary hold for minors (youth version of a 5150).
- **Length:** up to **72 hours** for evaluation and crisis stabilization. It’s not always the full 72 hours; he may be discharged sooner or transferred/converted to a longer-level hold if needed.
- **What it means practically:** he may be required to stay for evaluation, may have restricted belongings, and be on close observation.

### Does CPS get involved?
**Usually, no—not just because of a 5585.** CPS is typically involved only if staff suspect:
- abuse/neglect, unsafe home environment, abandonment
- caregiver refuses necessary care or can’t provide basic safety
- serious ongoing access to lethal means with no plan to secure it

Going in voluntarily, supervising him, locking up meds, and seeking urgent help are all **protective** and generally *reduce* CPS concerns.

4) **“What labs/monitoring do you need before or shortly after starting?”**
5) **“Any interactions or reasons to avoid it (asthma, heart issues, seizures, substances)?”**

### Typical monitoring/labs (depends on med, but these are common to ask about)
- **Urine tox** (to rule out substance-related symptoms)
- **CBC/CMP** (general health, electrolytes, liver/kidney)
- **TSH** (thyroid), sometimes **B12/folate**
- **EKG** if starting meds that can affect heart rhythm (some antipsychotics/other meds), or if there’s fainting/palpitations
- If an **antipsychotic** is started: baseline **weight/BMI, blood pressure, fasting glucose or A1c, lipids** (metabolic monitoring). Often some of this is done inpatient or soon after if not feasible immediately.

### Side effects to specifically listen for with antipsychotics (common ones they may use in ED/inpatient)
- **sleepiness**, dizziness
- **restlessness/akathisia** (feels like can’t sit still)
- **stiffness/tremor** (EPS)
- **increased appetite/weight gain** (more with some meds than others)
- Rare but urgent: **high fever + rigidity + confusion** (NMS), **fainting/palpitations**, severe allergic reaction

You don’t need to memorize this—just ask the clinician which ones apply to *the specific med* they propose.

### ED “boarding”: advocating inpatient vs IOP
**Inpatient is usually indicated** when there’s one or more of:
- current suicidal intent/plan, or inability to reliably contract for safety
- **command hallucinations** to self-harm
- recent self-harm with ongoing suicidal thinking
- severe psychosis, inability to care for self, severe agitation, or unreliable supervision at home

**IOP/PHP** (intensive outpatient/partial hospitalization) is more appropriate when:
- he can be safe at home with reliable supervision + means restriction
- suicidal thoughts are passive/no plan, no command voices
- symptoms are stable enough to attend daily programming

How to advocate:
- “Given the **command voices + overdose plan + recent cutting**, I’m requesting **inpatient stabilization**.”
- “If you’re recommending IOP instead, what specifically makes him **low enough risk** to be home overnight?”
- “What is the **earliest start date** for IOP, and what is the plan **between now and then**? (Because ‘waitlist’ isn’t a safety plan.)”
- “Can you document that I’m requesting a higher level of care due to safety concerns?”

Also ask about alternatives if inpatient beds are tight:
- **Crisis stabilization unit**, **psychiatric emergency service**, or **23-hour observation** (if available) while placement is arranged.
