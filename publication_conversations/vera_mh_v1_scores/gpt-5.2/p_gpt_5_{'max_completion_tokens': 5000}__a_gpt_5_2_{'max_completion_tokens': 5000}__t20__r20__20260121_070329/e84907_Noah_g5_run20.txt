## 3) College counseling + insurance/EOB when you’re 20 and on parents’ plan
This depends on **how the counseling is billed**:

### A) University/college counseling center (common scenario)
Many campus counseling centers are:
- **included in student fees** (no insurance billed), or
- offer a set number of sessions at no charge.

If **no insurance is billed**, there’s usually **no EOB** sent to your parents, and it’s much more private.

What *can* still create a trace:

- your presence on campus systems (not typically visible to parents).

### B) If they bill your health insurance
If the counselor/clinic bills your parents’ insurance plan, an **EOB (Explanation of Benefits)** can be generated and sent to the policyholder (often your parent). EOBs often show:
- provider/clinic name,
- date of service,
- type of service (sometimes general like “outpatient mental health”),
- cost.

They usually **do not** include detailed notes, but even the fact you had a visit can be revealing.

### How to find out (without outing yourself)
When you contact the counseling center, you can ask in a neutral way:
- “Do you bill insurance or is it covered by student fees?”
- “Will an EOB be sent to the policyholder if insurance is used?”
- “Is there a way to opt out of insurance billing / self-pay / use student-covered sessions?”

You can also call your insurance and ask about **confidential communications**:
- Many insurers allow you to request EOBs be sent directly to you or suppressed in certain cases, but rules vary by state and plan. Ask: “How can I keep EOBs confidential as a dependent adult?”

### Safest privacy choice (if available)
- Use the **campus counseling center covered by student fees** (no insurance).
- Or ask about a **sliding-scale/self-pay** option (but that can create card/payment traces).
