## Why this can happen (and why it doesn’t mean you want it)
Sleep deprivation + postpartum hormonal shifts + stress + returning to work can cause:
- **Intrusive thoughts** (unwanted, repetitive, upsetting thoughts)
- **Postpartum depression/anxiety** (very common, very treatable)
- A kind of mental “alarm system” that misfires when you’re exhausted

These thoughts are symptoms, not truths. A good reframe is:
> “This is a stress thought, not a desire and not a plan.”

## Commute: make it safer and less triggering
Because your intrusive images involve traffic, treat commuting like a high-stress window and add supports:

### If you drive
- **Don’t drive if you’re severely sleep-deprived.** Sleepiness itself is a safety issue. If you feel too tired: ask your husband for a ride, use rideshare, public transit, or work from home if possible.
- **Plan a “no stopping on edges” route:** avoid walking near fast traffic; park closer; use crosswalks; give yourself extra time.
- Keep a grounding cue ready: cold water bottle, strong mint gum, textured keychain—something that pulls attention to the present.
- If a wave hits: **pull over** somewhere safe, both hands on the wheel, long exhale, label it (“intrusive thought”), wait for the peak to pass (usually minutes), then continue only when you feel steady.

### If you walk near traffic/transit
- Choose a route with **barriers** (inside parking garage, farther from curb).
- Stay on the **inside** of the sidewalk away from the street.
- If a wave hits: **stop**, step back from the curb, look at a fixed point, breathe out long.

These steps aren’t because you’re “at risk” from intent—they’re practical guardrails for a stressed, sleep-starved brain.
