## What to expect if MCOT (Mobile Crisis Outreach Team) comes
This varies by county/team, but generally:

- **Who shows up:** typically **2 clinicians** (often a licensed clinician + a qualified mental health professional). Sometimes a peer specialist accompanies.  
- **Uniforms/law enforcement:** usually **not police** and not uniforms like patrol; often casual/professional clothing with badges. Police may be requested only if there’s a safety risk or weapons.
- **Where the assessment happens:** in your home; they’ll try to choose the calmest, most private space.
- **Separate interviews:** commonly they’ll speak to **you first briefly**, then **your son alone** (if he agrees), then together. They’ll also ask you for collateral even if he doesn’t want to talk much.
- **What they ask:** suicidal thoughts/plan/intent, self-harm details, hallucinations content (commands?), sleep, substances (THC/caffeine), mood symptoms, trauma/stress, safety at home, and protective factors.
- **How long it takes:** often **60–120 minutes** total, sometimes longer depending on complexity and de-escalation needs.
- **What they can do:**  
  - make a **safety plan** (very concrete: supervision, coping steps, emergency contacts, means restriction)  
  - connect you to **next-day urgent appointments** (sometimes “bridge” clinics)  
  - advise level of care (outpatient vs IOP/PHP vs ER/inpatient)
  - **arrange ER transport** *if needed*, which can include coordinating with EMS/law enforcement depending on risk and his willingness. They generally **can’t physically force** a voluntary transport without going through the legal/involuntary process, but they can initiate the appropriate steps if he’s an imminent danger.

If you’re worried about the visit escalating him, you can tell dispatch ahead of time:  
**“He’s easily spooked—please arrive quietly, no sirens, plainclothes if possible.”**
