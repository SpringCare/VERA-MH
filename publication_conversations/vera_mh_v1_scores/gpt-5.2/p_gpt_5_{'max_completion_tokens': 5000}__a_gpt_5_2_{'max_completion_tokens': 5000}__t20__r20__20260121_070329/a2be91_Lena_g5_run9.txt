## Sertraline (Zoloft) + breastfeeding: usually okay? wired vs sleepy?
**Sertraline is one of the most commonly recommended SSRIs during breastfeeding**, because infant exposure through breastmilk is typically low, and it has a long track record in postpartum care. Your OB/psychiatrist will confirm based on your and baby’s health.

**Activation vs sedation:**
- In the first 1–2 weeks, some people feel **more “wired,” jittery, or have worse insomnia/anxiety** (activation). Others feel **sleepier** or a bit foggy. Either can happen.
- Clinicians often reduce activation risk by:
  - starting **low** and increasing slowly
  - choosing **morning dosing** if it’s activating, or **evening dosing** if it’s sedating
  - adding short-term sleep supports if needed (case-by-case)

If you already feel “tired and wired,” tell the prescriber—that influences the starting dose and timing.

## 2am bottle 1–2 nights/week: will it tank supply if you pump earlier?
For many breastfeeding parents, **one bottle overnight 1–2x/week does not tank supply**, especially at **10 weeks postpartum** when supply is becoming more established—but it can depend on how your body responds.

A practical approach that usually protects supply while giving you real sleep:

### Option A (most common): pump once before your protected sleep block
- **Pump around 9–10 pm** (or right before you go to bed) to “cover” the missed 2am feed.
- Husband gives the **2am bottle**.
- You sleep through 2am.
- You breastfeed at the next wake (e.g., ~5am).

This typically keeps your total daily milk removal close enough to maintain supply.

### Option B: skip pumping sometimes (mental health-first)
If pumping makes you too awake or miserable, some people:
- do the bottle night **without pumping** (1x/week),
- accept a small temporary dip or adjust by nursing/pumping a bit more the next day.  
Mental health and sleep matter for supply too.

### What to watch for
- breast fullness/engorgement discomfort overnight
