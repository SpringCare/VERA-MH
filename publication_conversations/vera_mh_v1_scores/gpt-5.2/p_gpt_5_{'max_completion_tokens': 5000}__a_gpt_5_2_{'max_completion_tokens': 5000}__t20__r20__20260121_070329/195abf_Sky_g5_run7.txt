## Calc: implicit differentiation
Given:
\[
x^2y + \sin y = 3
\]

Differentiate both sides w.r.t. \(x\):

1) Differentiate \(x^2y\) using product rule:
\[
\frac{d}{dx}(x^2y)=2xy + x^2\frac{dy}{dx}
\]

2) Differentiate \(\sin y\) using chain rule:
\[
\frac{d}{dx}(\sin y)=\cos y \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}
\]

3) Differentiate 3:
\[
\frac{d}{dx}(3)=0
\]

So:
\[
2xy + x^2\frac{dy}{dx} + \cos y \frac{dy}{dx} = 0
\]

Factor \(\frac{dy}{dx}\):
\[
2xy + \left(x^2 + \cos y\right)\frac{dy}{dx} = 0
\]

Solve:
\[
\left(x^2 + \cos y\right)\frac{dy}{dx} = -2xy
\]
\[
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-2xy}{x^2 + \cos y}
\]

Where yours went off: you treated the derivative of the left side like it stayed “3 − sin y” on top. But when differentiating, the constant 3 becomes 0, and \(\sin y\) becomes \(\cos y \cdot y'\).

### 3 practice types to nail (nested chain rule with exp/log)
Here are three *high-yield* templates that cover most of the “my brain fries” situations—do several of each until the steps feel automatic.

1) **Exponential with a complicated exponent**
   - Type: \(y = e^{f(x)}\) or \(y = a^{f(x)}\)

3) **“Logarithmic differentiation” for nasty powers**

   - Practice example: \(y = (x^2+1)^{\sin x}\)  
   Key move: take \(\ln\) both sides, then differentiate implicitly.
