## Sertraline (Zoloft): how long to help + breastfeeding safety
Yes—**sertraline is widely considered compatible with breastfeeding** and is commonly a first-choice SSRI postpartum.

### How long until it helps
Typical timeline (varies by person):
- **First 3–7 days:** sometimes subtle changes (less constant panic, slightly better steadiness), or side effects before benefits
- **2–4 weeks:** clearer improvement for anxiety/depression
- **6–8 weeks:** full effect for many people (sometimes longer)

Some people do feel meaningful relief earlier, but it’s best to expect a few weeks.

### Breastfeeding: “drowsy baby” risk?
Sertraline generally has **very low levels in breastmilk** and infant blood levels are usually minimal/undetectable. Most babies have **no noticeable effects**.

Things to *watch for* (uncommon, but worth knowing):
- unusual sleepiness/sedation
- feeding less well
- irritability or GI changes (looser stools)
If you notice those, you’d call the pediatrician and the prescribing clinician—often it’s still manageable (dose timing adjustments, monitoring, or changing meds if needed).

Also important: **untreated postpartum depression/anxiety** can affect sleep, bonding, functioning, and safety. For many families, treatment (including an SSRI if indicated) is a net benefit for both parent and baby.
