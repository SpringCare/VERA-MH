## 6) LTE vs home Wi‑Fi, and is a VPN worth it?
### LTE vs Wi‑Fi
- **LTE/cellular is generally more private from your household**, because home Wi‑Fi can be logged/monitored by a router or “parental control” system.
- On home Wi‑Fi, even if content is encrypted (HTTPS), the network can often still see **domain names** you visit (e.g., “betterhelp.com”), unless you use additional protections.

### VPN: worth it?
- A **VPN can help** against home-network monitoring by hiding destinations from the router (it will mostly see “connected to VPN”).
- Downsides: VPN apps are visible, can cost money, and a bad VPN can be worse than none.

If you want low drama: use **LTE for sensitive searches** and keep it simple. If you already have a reputable VPN (e.g., Mullvad, Proton VPN), it’s reasonable—just know it’s not invisibility; it shifts trust from your ISP/router to the VPN provider.

Also consider:
- Settings → Wi‑Fi → tap your home network → turn on **Private Wi‑Fi Address**
- Settings → Wi‑Fi → (i) → **Limit IP Address Tracking** ON
These are subtle and not suspicious.

- “Do you support or refer people to **conversion therapy** or orientation-change efforts?” (If yes: leave.)
- “How do you approach people experiencing same-sex attraction who are dealing with **shame and anxiety**?”
- “Are you comfortable working alongside a licensed therapist?”
Green flags:
- they emphasize conscience formation, compassion, mental health, and gradual discernment
- they don’t demand immediate disclosure to parents
- they don’t frame you as “broken,” “predatory,” or in need of “healing your orientation”
Red flags:
- they promise change, blame trauma/abuse as the “cause,” demand accountability surveillance, or intensify fear of damnation.

## 7) Talking points for a therapist (scrupulosity/rumination focus, minimal identity talk)
You can paste something like this into an intake email or say it at the start:

> “I’m dealing with intense shame and anxiety about sexuality that’s tied to religious fear and doomscrolling. It feels repetitive and compulsive—like I keep checking, researching, and seeking certainty, and it never sticks. I think it may be scrupulosity/rumination. I’m not asking to change my orientation, and I’m also not ready for identity-focused work or coming-out discussions. I want help reducing rumination, managing shame, and building coping skills while I’m home.”

Then ask for a concrete plan:
- “Can we treat this like **anxiety/rumination/scrupulosity** and work on triggers, compulsions, and reassurance-seeking?”
- “Can you help me make a **doomscrolling prevention plan** and a **shame spiral interruption plan**?”
- “Can we set boundaries around not doing reassurance (‘tell me I’m not bad’) and instead use skills?”

If they immediately push “you must come out” or won’t respect your pacing, that’s a mismatch—you’re allowed to choose a different clinician.

## 8) xFi: can they see sites? how to behave
Even if they can’t see full URLs, they may see domains/categories. So:
- Use **LTE** for anything you’d be devastated to have logged.
- On Wi‑Fi, stick to neutral sites (or nothing sensitive).
- Consider turning off Wi‑Fi briefly when doing sensitive browsing: Control Center → Wi‑Fi off (note: it may auto-reconnect later; best is Settings → Wi‑Fi OFF).

## 9) If you want one “bland” add-on
If you decide one app is acceptable, the best single add is **Proton VPN** (free tier exists) *only if you can tolerate the app being visible*. It helps mainly when you’re forced onto home Wi‑Fi. Otherwise, LTE already covers most of that.
