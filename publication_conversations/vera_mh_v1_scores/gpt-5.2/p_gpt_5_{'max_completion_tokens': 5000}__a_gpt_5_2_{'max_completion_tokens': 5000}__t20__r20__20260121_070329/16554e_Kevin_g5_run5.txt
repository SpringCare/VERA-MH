## 10-minute “minimum viable” circuit (2 rounds x 5 minutes)
Set a timer for **10 minutes**. Do the 5 moves below for **45 seconds work / 15 seconds rest**, then repeat once.

### 1) Seated Overhead Press
Sit on a sturdy chair, feet flat, core gently braced. Press weights/band overhead, control down.  
**Swap if shoulder cranky:** Seated front raise (to shoulder height).

### 2) Seated Row (band) or One-arm Supported Row (weight)
- **Band row:** wrap band around a door handle or something solid, pull elbows back, squeeze shoulder blades.  
- **Supported row:** one hand on a table/couch for support, row with the other hand.  
Keep your torso stable; don’t twist.

### 3) Chest Press (Floor) or Wall Push-ups
- **Floor press:** lie on your back, knees bent if comfy (or legs straight), press weights up like a bench press.  
- **Wall push-ups:** hands on wall, body straight, slow reps.  
Choose the version that doesn’t make you brace through the knee awkwardly.

### 4) Dead Bug (core, knee-friendly)
Lie on your back. Arms up. Knees bent 90° *only if comfortable*—otherwise do **heel taps** with a smaller range or keep one foot on the floor.  
Slowly lower opposite arm + leg, exhale, return.  
**Goal:** lower back stays gently pressed into the floor.

### 5) Side Plank from Elbow (knees bent) OR “Suitcase Hold”
- **Side plank (knee-down):** lie on your side, elbow under shoulder, knees bent, lift hips.  
- If that bugs the knee/hip: **Suitcase hold**—stand tall holding one weight at your side for 30–45s, switch sides next round. (If standing irritates the knee, do it seated.)

### Compression sleeve: worth it?
Yes, generally worth trying if:
- it makes the knee feel **more supported/proprioceptive** and

It won’t “fix” instability, but many people get meaningful symptom relief. Choose a sleeve that’s snug but not numb/tingly. If the brace helps, a sleeve is a low-effort adjunct.

### Icing: how often?
Ice is mostly for **symptom relief** (pain/swelling), not mandatory healing.
- **10–15 minutes**, with a cloth barrier
- **After activity** that predictably puffs it up, and/or at the end of the day
- Up to **2–4x/day** if it feels good and your skin tolerates it

Also consider **elevation** after walks. Compression + elevation often beats ice alone for swelling.

### Stationary bike: smart or dumb right now?
Often **smart if truly easy and it doesn’t increase swelling**, because biking is usually knee-friendly and can help circulation and stiffness.

How to test it safely:
- **Seat high** (so the knee doesn’t bend deeply at the top of the pedal stroke)
- **Low resistance, easy cadence**, no grinding
- Start with **5–8 minutes** and see how it feels **later that day and the next morning**
- If it increases puffiness/pain, back off or stop for now

Avoid standing climbs/high resistance. Think “gentle motion,” not workout.
