### CPS
- Seeking emergency care for your child and asking questions about meds is **not** what triggers CPS.
- CPS concerns generally arise when there is suspected **abuse/neglect** (e.g., refusal of necessary care in a way that places the child at serious risk, ongoing lack of supervision, unsafe home environment).
- Saying “I’m not comfortable with olanzapine; can we try hydroxyzine first / can you explain risks / can we do EKG” is normal advocacy.
- If clinicians believe a medication is urgently necessary to prevent imminent harm and a parent refuses, they may involve hospital legal/ethics processes—but that’s usually in more extreme, immediate-danger scenarios.

## A concise decision aid you can use with the clinician (30 seconds)
You can say:
> “He’s had 1–2 hours sleep for 3 nights and is pacing. If you think this is possible first-episode psychosis or mania, I understand why olanzapine might help more than hydroxyzine. What’s the lowest one-time dose you recommend, what monitoring will you do, and do you want an EKG first? I’m okay with what’s safest and most likely to stabilize him tonight.”

If you tell me the **exact olanzapine dose** they’re proposing (2.5 vs 5 vs 10 mg) and whether they’re also giving anything else, I can help you refine questions around monitoring and interaction risk.
