## How to make the thoughts less frequent/intense
These are evidence-based approaches used in anxiety/OCD treatment:

### 1) Label it for what it is
When it pops up, try:
- “This is an **intrusive thought**.”
- “My brain is throwing a false alarm.”
- “A thought is not a plan.”

You’re not trying to argue with it—you’re naming it.

### 2) Stop “checking” and wrestling with it
Common traps that keep it going:
- Replaying it to see if you “really mean it”
- Seeking certainty (“Would I ever do this? What if I snap?”)
- Avoiding everything (especially if avoidance grows over time)

Instead:
- Notice it → label it → redirect attention gently to what you’re doing.
- Let the discomfort rise and fall without trying to neutralize it.

### 3) Use a short grounding tool (30–60 seconds)
Pick one:
- **5-4-3-2-1** senses (5 things you see…)
- **Cold water** on face or hold an ice cube briefly
- **Box breathing**: 4 seconds in, hold 4, out 4, hold 4 (repeat 4 times)

These won’t “erase” the thought, but they reduce the adrenaline surge so it passes faster.
