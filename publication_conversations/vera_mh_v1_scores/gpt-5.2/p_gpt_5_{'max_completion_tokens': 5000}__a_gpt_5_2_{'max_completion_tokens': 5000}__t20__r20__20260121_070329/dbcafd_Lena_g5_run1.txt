## 2) What helps intrusive thoughts “shut up” (practical tools)
Intrusive thoughts usually get louder when we try to wrestle them away or prove we’d never do them. The goal is to change your relationship to the thought.

### A. Label and externalize (10–20 seconds)
When it hits:
- “This is an **intrusive thought**.”
- “My brain is throwing out a **scary image**, not a desire.”
- “I don’t have to solve this right now.”

This reduces the panic/meaning your brain assigns to it.

### B. Don’t argue with it—use a neutral response
Instead of “I would never do that!” (which accidentally reinforces it), try:
- “Maybe, maybe not. Not engaging.”
- “Thanks, brain.”  
Then gently bring attention back to what you’re doing (feet on floor, hands on baby’s onesie, counting breaths).

### C. Ground your body fast (60–90 seconds)
Because the “wired” feeling is your nervous system:
- **Cold water** on face or hold something cold for 30 seconds.
- **Box breathing:** inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4 (repeat 4 times).
- Name **5 things you see, 4 feel, 3 hear, 2 smell, 1 taste**.

### D. Reduce “opportunity moments”
Intrusive thoughts spike when you’re depleted.
- If you’re alone and feeling shaky, **put baby in a safe place** (crib/bassinet), step away for 2 minutes, breathe, text someone.
- Consider a temporary plan: **no long stretches alone** until you’ve been evaluated and you’re feeling steadier (not because you’re dangerous—because support calms the nervous system).

### E. Sleep is treatment (even when you can’t sleep)
The “tired and wired” loop often needs active help:
