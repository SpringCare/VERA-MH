## How do you get a 3–4 hour sleep block with feeds every 2–3 hours?
It’s hard—but there are a few workable patterns, even while breastfeeding. The goal is not perfect sleep; it’s **one protected stretch** most days for your brain to come down from the ledge.

### Option A: “Husband on duty” + you only wake to feed (minimum disruption)
- Husband is responsible for **everything except the nursing**: diaper, resettle, rocking, getting baby to/from you, burping, putting baby back down.
- You stay in a dark room, minimal talking/light/phone.
Even if you nurse every 2–3 hours, cutting out the “fully wake up + do all tasks” part can meaningfully help.

### Option B: One bottle so you can skip one feed (often the most effective)
If you’re willing/able (and your pediatrician/lactation consultant agrees):
- Pick one time window (often early night, e.g., 9 pm–1 am).
- Husband gives **one bottle** (pumped milk or formula).
- You sleep through that feed to get a **3–4 hour block**.

Many people worry this will “ruin breastfeeding.” For lots of families, one protected bottle is the difference between coping and not coping. If supply is a concern, some people add a brief pump earlier in the day or after the long sleep—your lactation consultant can tailor it.

### Option C: “Cluster-feed then protected nap”
- You feed more frequently for a couple hours in the evening (if baby naturally does this), then hand off to husband for the longest first stretch.
This sometimes buys a longer initial sleep.

### Option D: If nights are impossible, get the block in daylight
If your husband can take a morning shift before work or after:
- You take a **3-hour daytime sleep** while he does baby care (and brings baby only for nursing, if needed).

## How fast do ERP and meds help?

### SSRIs (if prescribed)

- If sleep is severely impaired, some clinicians add a short-term bridge plan (individualized, especially while breastfeeding).
