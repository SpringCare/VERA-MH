## 1) \(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{3^n}{n!}\)
**Fast pick:** factorial in the denominator → **Ratio test** is usually best.

Let \(a_n=\frac{3^n}{n!}\).
\[
\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|
= \frac{3^{n+1}/(n+1)!}{3^n/n!}
= \frac{3}{n+1}\to 0
\]
Since the limit \(L=0<1\), the series **converges absolutely**.

(Also it’s \(e^3\), but you don’t need that.)

## 2) \(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n\ln n}{n}\)
**Fast pick:** alternating sign \( (-1)^n\) → try **Alternating Series Test (Leibniz)** first, then check absolute convergence.

Let \(b_n=\frac{\ln n}{n}\) (positive terms).
Alternating Series Test needs:
1) \(b_n \to 0\): yes, \(\ln n / n \to 0\).
2) \(b_n\) decreasing eventually: yes for \(n\) large (you can justify via derivative of \(f(x)=\ln x/x\), which is \((1-\ln x)/x^2<0\) for \(x>e\)).

So the series **converges** by AST.

**Absolute convergence?** Consider \(\sum \frac{\ln n}{n}\).
This diverges (e.g., **integral test**: \(\int (\ln x)/x\,dx = \tfrac12(\ln x)^2\) blows up).
Therefore the original alternating series is **conditionally convergent**.

## 3) \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n}{n^2+1}\)
**Fast pick:** looks like \(\frac{n}{n^2} \approx \frac{1}{n}\) → compare to harmonic.

Let \(a_n=\frac{n}{n^2+1}\).
For large \(n\), \(a_n \sim \frac{1}{n}\). A clean comparison:
\[
\frac{n}{n^2+1} \ge \frac{n}{n^2+n^2} = \frac{1}{2n}\quad (\text{for }n\ge 1)
\]
Since \(\sum \frac{1}{2n}\) diverges (harmonic), by **comparison test** the given series **diverges**.

(You could also do limit comparison with \(1/n\): \(\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n/(n^2+1)}{1/n}=\lim \frac{n^2}{n^2+1}=1\).)

# How to choose a test fast (a “pattern matcher”)
When you freeze, scan in this order:

1) **Does \(a_n \not\to 0\)?**  
Compute \(\lim a_n\). If not 0 → **diverges immediately** (nth-term test).

2) **Is it geometric or p-series (or algebraic tweak of one)?**  
- Geometric: \(ar^n\) → converges if \(|r|<1\)  
- \( \sum 1/n^p\) → converges if \(p>1\)

3) **Alternating \( (-1)^n\) or \(\cos(\pi n)\) etc.?**  
Try **Alternating Series Test** (check decreasing + limit 0). Then check absolute convergence if asked.

4) **Factorials or exponentials?**  
- Factorial \(n!\) / products like \((2n)(2n-1)\): **Ratio test**  
- Powers like \(c^n\) also often ratio/root

5) **n-th roots / something like \((\cdot)^n\)**  
Try **Root test**.

6) **It looks like a rational function in \(n\)** (polynomials top/bottom)  
Compare to \(1/n^p\) by highest powers:  
\(\frac{n^k}{n^m} \sim 1/n^{m-k}\). Use **comparison/limit comparison**.

7) **Logs or weird slowly varying things** (\(\ln n\), \((\ln n)^p\), etc.)  
Often **integral test** or comparison to known benchmarks (\(1/n\), \(1/(n(\ln n)^p)\)).
