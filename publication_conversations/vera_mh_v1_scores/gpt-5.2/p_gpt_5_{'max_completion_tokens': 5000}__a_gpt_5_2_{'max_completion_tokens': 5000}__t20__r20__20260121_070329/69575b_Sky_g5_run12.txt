## Handling IG / TikTok comments (without making it worse)
Harassers *want* engagement. The safest approach is usually: **don’t reply, restrict visibility, quietly report, and tighten settings**.

### Instagram
1) **Limit Comments**: Settings → Privacy → Comments  
   - Turn on **“Hide offensive comments”** and **“Advanced comment filtering.”**  
2) **Restrict** people (best low-drama option): go to their profile → **Restrict**  
   - They can comment, but it’s hidden unless you approve. They often don’t realize.
3) **Block** if needed (clean cut).  
4) **Filter keywords**: Privacy → Hidden Words

5) **Control DMs**: Privacy → Messages

### TikTok
1) Settings and privacy → **Privacy**  
   - **Comments**: set to Friends or No one (even temporarily).  
   - Turn on **Filter all comments** or **Filter keywords**.
2) **Block** accounts quickly; don’t warn them.
3) **Limit who can DM you**.

**Reporting making it worse:** it *can* sometimes escalate if they notice, but most reporting is not “announced” to them. What does reduce escalation risk is **not responding** and using **Restrict/filters** first so they don’t get feedback.

## Reporting to Student Conduct / Title IX: can it be anonymous?
It depends on your school, but generally:

- You can often **ask to be kept confidential**, but **true anonymity is hard** if the school needs to investigate and take action (they may need to disclose enough detail for the other side to respond).
- Many campuses let you **make an initial report without filing a formal complaint**, just to document and get options.
- Most Title IX offices can do an **intake meeting** where you ask:  
  “If I report this, what will the accused be told? Will my name be shared? What are the limits of confidentiality?”  
  You can ask these questions **before** giving names/details.

**Important nuance:** Title IX is usually for sex-based harassment/discrimination; anti-trans harassment often falls under Title IX at many schools, but sometimes it’s handled under an LGBTQ+/equity office or student conduct. Either way, you can still ask about confidentiality and retaliation protections.
