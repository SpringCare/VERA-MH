## What usually happens at the hospital (Texas; typical ER/psychiatric process)
1) **Triage + medical check**
   - They’ll check vitals and ask what brought you in: suicidal thoughts, self-harm, hearing voices.
   - They may do basic labs/urine tests (sometimes to rule out medical causes or substance involvement), and they’ll check any cuts if needed.

2) **Safety precautions**
   - For anyone suicidal, they often place the patient in a safer room and may ask them to change into hospital clothing and remove items that could be used to self-harm (belt, strings, phone charger, etc.).
   - Many ERs use **constant observation** (a staff member nearby). This can feel intense, but it’s for immediate safety.

3) **Mental health evaluation**
   - A psychiatrist, psychiatric nurse practitioner, or licensed mental health clinician will assess:
     - suicidal thoughts/intent/plan
     - self-harm history
     - the **voices** (what they say, how often, whether they’re “commanding”)
     - mood, sleep, substance use, stressors
     - supports at home and your ability to supervise
   - They’ll also talk with you separately to get your observations.

4) **Disposition (the “what happens next” decision)**
   - **Option A: Discharge home with a safety plan** if they believe he can be kept safe with supervision and quick follow-up. They may arrange urgent outpatient psychiatry/therapy and sometimes start medication.
   - **Option B: Short-term inpatient psychiatric hospitalization** if they think he’s at significant risk or cannot commit to safety, or the psychosis symptoms are severe. This is not punishment—it’s stabilization and safety.
