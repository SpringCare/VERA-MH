### How to stop thoughts from taking over (practical skills)
These are used for intrusive thoughts, shame spirals, and OCD-like patterns:

#### 1) Label the thought, don’t argue with it
Instead of “I’m disgusting” / “This is a sin” / “I have to stop,” try:
- “I’m having the thought that I’m disgusting.”
- “My brain is sending the ‘sin’ alarm again.”
This creates a tiny bit of distance so it has less power.

#### 2) Drop the struggle (paradoxically reduces frequency)
When the thought shows up, do a short script:
- “Okay, there’s that thought.”
- “I don’t have to solve it right now.”
- “I’m going to put my attention on what I’m doing.”
The goal isn’t to approve of the thought—it’s to stop fighting it like an emergency.

#### 3) Do a 60–90 second body reset
Pick one:
- **Box breathing:** inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4 (x4 rounds)
- **Cold water:** splash face or hold something cold for 30 seconds
- **Grounding 5–4–3–2–1:** 5 things you see, 4 feel, 3 hear, 2 smell, 1 taste  
This helps your nervous system come down so the thought doesn’t feel catastrophic.

#### 4) “Worry time” / containment
If you keep ruminating at night:
- Set a daily 10-minute “rumination window” earlier in the day.
- When thoughts hit later: “Not now—at 6:30pm.” Write one line in a note and return to your task.
This trains your brain that it doesn’t get unlimited airtime.

#### 5) Cut the shame loop: thought → meaning → punishment
A common pattern is:
- Thought/attraction → “I’m bad” → self-disgust/punishment → more stress → more intrusive thoughts.
You can interrupt it with a rule:
- **No self-insults.** Even if you believe it’s a sin, attacking yourself fuels the cycle and makes thoughts more frequent.

#### 6) If you’re doing “checking” behaviors, reduce them
Examples: mentally reviewing, praying repetitively to “cancel” thoughts, confessing repeatedly, googling “am I gay,” testing arousal, avoiding all men, etc. Those can work like compulsions—short-term relief, long-term stronger thoughts.
