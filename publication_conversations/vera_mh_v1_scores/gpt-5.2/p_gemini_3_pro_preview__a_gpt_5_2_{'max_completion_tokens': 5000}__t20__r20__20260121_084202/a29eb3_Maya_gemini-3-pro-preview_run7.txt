- Peanut butter on toast
- Cottage cheese + fruit
- Popcorn + a protein (string cheese / turkey)

- Keep tempting foods **out of sight** or not in the house for a while if possible.
- Pre-portion snacks (single bowls/bags).
- “Kitchen closed” routine: clean up, lights off, **brush/floss**, minty mouthwash (surprisingly effective).
- If you’re eating in front of screens, try a rule: **food only at the table**.

### 5) Reduce the “tired = hungry” effect
Sleep loss strongly increases appetite and cravings.
- Try a consistent bedtime/wake time for a week.
- If you’re up late, you’ll be more likely to snack—sometimes the fix is simply **go to bed earlier**.

### 6) Manage stress/feelings without food (a short menu)
Night eating is often emotional regulation. Pick 2–3 go-to options:
- Hot shower / skincare routine
- Journaling for 5 minutes (“what am I feeling?”)
- A short walk or gentle stretching
- Call/text someone
- Low-effort comfort: blanket + show + tea

### 7) Be careful with “all-or-nothing” rules
If you ban all night eating, one slip can turn into “might as well.” A better rule:
- “If I eat at night, it will be **a planned portion**, at the table, no screens.”

### 8) When to consider getting extra support
If you **wake up to eat**, feel out of control around food, binge, purge, restrict heavily during the day, or this is tied to depression/anxiety, it may be worth talking with a clinician or a dietitian—these patterns are very treatable.
