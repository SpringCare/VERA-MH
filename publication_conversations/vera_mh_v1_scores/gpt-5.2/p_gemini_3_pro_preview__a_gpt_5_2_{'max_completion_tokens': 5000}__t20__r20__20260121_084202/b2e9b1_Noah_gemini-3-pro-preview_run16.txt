chatbot: CBT and ACT aren’t about turning you into a “cheerleader” for anything. They’re structured treatments for *distress*—the panic, shame, rumination, and self-hate that are making life feel unbearable.

### CBT vs ACT (plain language)
**CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy)**  
- Focus: how thoughts, feelings, and behaviors feed each other.  
- What you do: identify patterns like catastrophizing (“I’m broken forever”), mind-reading (“my family will definitely reject me”), all‑or‑nothing thinking (“normal or disgusting”), and safety behaviors (checking, reassurance seeking, endless arguing with yourself).  
- Goal: reduce anxiety/depression by changing the cycle—often by **changing responses** to thoughts and testing beliefs in real life.

**ACT (Acceptance & Commitment Therapy)**  
- “Acceptance” here does **not** mean “approve,” “like,” or “choose.” It means: *stop fighting the thought in a way that makes it louder*.  
- ACT teaches skills to notice a thought (“I’m broken”) as a mental event, not a fact, so it has less power. Then you focus on **values-based action** (what kind of person you want to be day-to-day).

**Will they stop the thoughts?**  
They often reduce how *sticky* and frequent thoughts are, but the bigger win is they make thoughts **less distressing and less controlling**, which usually leads to fewer spirals. The aim is relief and functioning—not forcing you to come out, and not forcing you to adopt an identity.

If your experience is more like **intrusive thoughts + shame + mental checking**, you can also ask about **ERP (Exposure & Response Prevention)**, a very effective approach for rumination/obsessional anxiety.
