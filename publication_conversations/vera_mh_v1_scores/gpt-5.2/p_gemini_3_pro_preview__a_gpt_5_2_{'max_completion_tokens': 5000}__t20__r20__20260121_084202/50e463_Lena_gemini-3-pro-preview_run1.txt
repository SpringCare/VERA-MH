## What this can be (and what it usually means)
### 1) **Postpartum intrusive thoughts / postpartum anxiety (very common)**
- Thoughts are **unwanted**, upsetting, and you try to push them away.
- Often includes checking, avoiding things (stairs, knives, bath time), reassurance-seeking, or constant worry.
- You still know the thoughts are irrational or not “you.”

### 2) **Postpartum OCD (also treatable and not rare)**
- Intrusive thoughts plus **compulsions** (checking, mental rituals, avoidance).
- You may feel terrified you could “snap,” even though you *don’t want to*.

### 3) **Postpartum depression (also common)**
- More sadness, numbness, guilt, hopelessness, crying, sleep/appetite changes (beyond newborn sleep loss), feeling disconnected.

### 4) **Postpartum psychosis (rare, emergency)**
This is different: thoughts feel *real* or commanded, or you’re not sure what’s real. It can include hallucinations, delusions, severe agitation, not sleeping for days, extreme confusion, or rapid mood changes.

# How to reduce intrusive thoughts *right now* (not perfect, but can lower the volume)
These are skills used for postpartum anxiety/OCD and panic. The goal is **not** to force the thought away (that often makes it stick), but to reduce the spiral.

### 1) Name it, don’t fight it (30 seconds)
When a thought hits:
- “This is an **intrusive thought**.”
- “My brain is sending a **false alarm**.”
- “I don’t have to solve this right now.”

Then return attention to one small task (diaper, bottle, washing hands). Treat it like mental spam.

### 2) Slow the body first (2 minutes)
Try **physiological sigh** x 5:
- Inhale through nose
- A quick second small inhale on top
- Long slow exhale through mouth  
Repeat 5 times. This can downshift the panic/racing.

### 3) “Anchor + allow” (1–3 minutes)
- Put baby somewhere safe (crib/bassinet) if you need both hands.
- Press your feet into the floor and name **5 things you see / 4 feel / 3 hear / 2 smell / 1 taste**.
This helps your brain exit the “threat loop.”

### 4) Containment phrase (helps when thoughts are graphic)
Say (out loud if you can):
- “Not a prediction. Not a plan. Just a thought.”
- “I’m allowed to have a thought and not act on it.”

### 5) Don’t test yourself
Avoid doing things to “prove you’re safe” (like holding a knife to see how you feel) and avoid scary avoidance rituals. If driving triggers “crash the car” thoughts, **don’t drive today**.
