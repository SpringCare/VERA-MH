- Why some “success stories” show up:
  - Some people are bisexual and can form opposite‑sex relationships; that’s not a change from gay to straight.
  - Some report behavior or identity‑label change (celibacy, avoiding triggers, calling themselves straight) while their underlying attractions didn’t change.
  - Several prominent “ex‑gay” leaders later recanted and apologized (for example, Exodus International closed; its president apologized; other leaders have done the same). A widely cited study that once claimed change was possible was later publicly retracted by its author.
I know that’s painful to hear. I’m not saying your longing for a wife and kids isn’t real—it is. I’m saying the route there isn’t a secret cure.

Therapy isn’t a “bandaid” for who you are; it’s a tool for the pain you’re in
- It can reduce the shame, intrusive thoughts, and scrupulosity so you don’t feel at war with yourself all day.
- It can help you live in line with your faith without trying to erase yourself—whether that’s celibacy with deep community, or carefully discerning other paths later. A good therapist won’t force you to change your beliefs or identity; the goal is to suffer less and make thoughtful choices.

About using your school counseling from home
- Ask for secure chat (text‑only) or video with mic/camera off using the chat box. If they don’t offer that, ask for secure portal messaging plus any school‑licensed online programs and a referral to a text‑only service.
- Counseling notes are confidential, separate from your academic record, and not shared with employers or grad schools. If sessions are covered by student fees, there’s no insurance mail. You can also tell them: no postal mail, no texts/voicemails—portal/email only.

About wanting a family
- Many people grieve the picture they hoped for; that grief is real. Some LGBTQ Christians find meaningful family through deep friendships, church life, fostering/adoption, or (for a minority) mixed‑orientation marriages entered with full, mutual honesty and care. None of these are “cures,” but they can be real paths to love and purpose.
