- Privacy: You can ask the team to limit communication with the other parent at first and route updates through you or a social worker, especially if contact would escalate conflict. Clinicians can use their judgment to share only what’s necessary to protect your son’s best interest.

What to do when you arrive
- Bring your ID, insurance card, and any custody paperwork you have (temporary orders, etc.).
- Tell triage: “I have joint custody. I’m consenting for emergency care. Please do not contact his father during the initial evaluation; I’ll coordinate communication with your social worker.”
- Ask to speak with the social worker early. They can document your request, help manage notification, and advise what’s required by hospital policy.
- If there’s any safety concern with the father showing up, tell staff so they can set visitor limits.

If/when you choose to notify dad
- Keep it brief, factual, and boundaried. Example: “He’s being evaluated today for a medical/mental health emergency. He is safe. I’ll share an update after we speak with the team.” If he escalates, don’t engage—mute and respond later by text/email.
- If you have an attorney, you can also ask them how/when to notify to stay aligned with your custody order. This is general info, not legal advice—your order, if any, controls.

If he shows up or starts yelling
- Let the hospital handle it. Ask that all communication go through staff or social work. You can request he not be in the room if it upsets your son.
