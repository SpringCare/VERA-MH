Handle urges in the moment
- Check a hunger scale (0–10). If you’re 6+ (stomach hungry), have the planned snack. If it’s more of a craving (3–5), try:

  - Brush teeth or use strong mouthwash
  - Do something brief and absorbing: shower, short walk, stretch, puzzle, call a friend
  - Keep hands busy (fold laundry, doodle)
- Keep trigger foods out of sight; pre‑portion anything you do keep.

Troubleshooting
- If you’re always ravenous at night, you’re likely under‑eating protein/calories earlier or eating too early a dinner. Shift more calories and protein to breakfast/lunch or eat dinner a bit later.

- If you prefer not to eat at night at all, use low‑calorie fillers: sparkling water, herbal tea, sugar‑free gum; consider a fiber supplement like psyllium with dinner if your doctor says it’s okay.

When to get extra help
- If you wake up to eat, skip breakfast due to morning loss of appetite, or feel out of control/binge at night, look into night eating syndrome or binge eating—both are treatable. A primary care clinician, registered dietitian, or therapist (CBT‑E/DBT) can help.
