Yes, you should expect and push for a thorough medical workup to rule out any underlying physical causes or complications of his state.

*   **Labs:**
    *   **Complete Blood Count (CBC):** Checks for infection, anemia.
    *   **Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP):** Checks kidney/liver function, electrolytes, blood sugar (important for medication metabolism and general health).
    *   **Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH):** Thyroid dysfunction can mimic psychiatric symptoms.
    *   **Inflammatory markers (e.g., CRP, ESR):** Can indicate inflammation or autoimmune issues affecting the brain.
    *   **Vitamin levels (e.g., B12, Folate):** Deficiencies can affect mental state.
*   **EKG (Electrocardiogram):** Essential to check heart function, especially before administering certain psych medications that can affect cardiac rhythm (like some antipsychotics).
*   **Urine Drug Screen (UDS):** Crucial to rule out substance-induced psychosis.
*   **Potentially a Head CT or MRI:** If there's any suspicion of head injury, neurological issues, or other structural brain problems contributing to his symptoms.

**Your line:** "Given his acute psychotic state and severe sleep deprivation, I expect a full medical workup including CBC, CMP, TSH, EKG, and a urine drug screen, to rule out any underlying medical causes or complications before psychiatric medication is fully initiated."

### **3. Side Effects to Watch For (Haldol/Ativan, Zyprexa) and Combinations:**

These are critical to know. You're right to be vigilant.

*   **General for all sedating meds:**
    *   **Respiratory Depression:** Shallow, slow breathing, difficulty waking up. This is an emergency.
    *   **Excessive Sedation:** Unresponsive, cannot be easily roused.
    *   **Low Blood Pressure:** Dizziness, fainting.
*   **Haldol (Haloperidol) - an antipsychotic:**
    *   **Akathisia:** Severe internal restlessness, inability to sit still, fidgeting, pacing. Can be very distressing and mistaken for agitation.
    *   **Dystonia:** Involuntary muscle contractions causing repetitive or twisting movements, abnormal postures (e.g., neck twisting, eye rolling). **Acute dystonia is an emergency.**
    *   **Parkinsonism:** Tremors, stiffness, shuffling gait.
    *   **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Rare but life-threatening. Fever, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic dysfunction (e.g., sweating, unstable blood pressure).
*   **Ativan (Lorazepam) - a benzodiazepine:**
    *   **Excessive Sedation/Drowsiness:** Can be pronounced.
    *   **Respiratory Depression:** Especially if given with other sedatives.
    *   **Paradoxical Excitation:** Rarely, can cause increased agitation or aggression instead of calming.
*   **Zyprexa (Olanzapine) - an antipsychotic:**
    *   **Sedation/Drowsiness:** Very common.
    *   **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Dizziness when standing up.
    *   **Weight gain/Metabolic changes:** More long-term concern, but good to know.
    *   **Akathisia/Dystonia:** Less common than with Haldol, but still possible.

*   **Mixing Meds (Benzos and Olanzapine IM):**
    *   **DO NOT mix IM Olanzapine and IM Benzodiazepines (like Ativan) within a short timeframe (usually within an hour or two).** This combination can significantly increase the risk of severe respiratory depression, excessive sedation, and cardiovascular collapse.
    *   **Your line if this comes up:** "Are you administering IM olanzapine? Please ensure there is no concurrent or recent administration of IM benzodiazepines due to the risk of respiratory depression." (They should know this, but it doesn't hurt to be aware and ask).

### **5. Staying with Him / Suicide Precautions / Restraints:**

*   **Can you stay with him in the ER?** **YES, absolutely push to stay with him.** As his mother and legal guardian, especially with a minor in a psychiatric crisis, your presence is crucial for his comfort and advocacy. "I need to stay with my son. My presence is crucial for his well-being and to ensure consistent care."
*   **Suicide Precautions/Constant Observation vs. Restraints:**
    *   **Your line:** "I understand the need for safety. I request **constant 1:1 observation** and comprehensive **suicide precautions** as the primary safety measures, rather than physical restraints, unless absolutely medically necessary for his immediate safety or the safety of others. Physical restraints can be traumatizing and escalate agitation in a psychotic state."
    *   **Why this is important:** Constant observation (a dedicated staff member watching him) and suicide precautions (removing all dangerous items, safe room) are often sufficient and less traumatizing than physical restraints. Restraints should be a last resort.
