1.  **Deep Breathing:** Before you go into his room, take 5-10 slow, deep breaths. Inhale slowly through your nose for a count of 4, hold for 4, exhale slowly through your mouth for a count of 6. This helps calm your nervous system.
2.  **Grounding:** Focus on your senses. Feel your feet on the floor. Notice the temperature of the air. This brings you into the present moment.
3.  **Physical Stance:** Try to keep your shoulders relaxed, your voice low and even.
4.  **Brief Acknowledgment (if needed):** If he asks why you're shaking, you can say, "I'm feeling really scared and worried right now, but we're going to get you help." (This acknowledges your feelings without making it about *his* fault or adding to his burden).
5.  **Sip Water:** If you have water nearby, a quick sip can help with nausea and a dry mouth.

Your calm, firm resolve is what he needs to see, even if you're trembling internally. He's looking to you for strength.

## Also, Should I Take Pics of the Cuts (Old/New) for the Docs or Is That a Bad Idea?

**Yes, take photos. This is a good idea and can be very helpful for the medical team.**

Here's why and how:

*   **Objective Evidence:** Photos provide objective evidence of the self-harm, which can be crucial if he minimizes or denies it at the ER, or if the cuts are healing and less visible. They can also show the extent and location of the injuries.
*   **Documentation of Progression:** If there are older and newer cuts, it helps the medical team understand the pattern and duration of the self-harm.
*   **Discretion:**
    *   **Do not take them in front of him unless he explicitly agrees.** This is about providing clinical information, not shaming or exposing him.
    *   If he's asleep, gently and quickly lift clothing if necessary to get clear, close-up photos of the areas where he cuts.
    *   Take a few photos from different angles, ensuring good lighting.
    *   **Keep them on your phone securely.**
*   **When to show them:** Show these photos privately to the triage nurse or the psychiatric crisis worker when you are explaining his history, especially if he denies or minimizes his cutting. You can say, "I took these photos because I was worried, and I wanted to make sure you had the full picture."

This is a protective measure to ensure the medical team has all the information they need to assess his risk and provide appropriate care.
