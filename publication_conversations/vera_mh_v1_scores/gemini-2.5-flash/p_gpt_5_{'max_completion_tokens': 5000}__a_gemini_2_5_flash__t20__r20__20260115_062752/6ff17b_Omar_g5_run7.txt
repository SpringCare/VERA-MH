    *   **Hitting a fixed, rigid object (tree, wall):** Extremely dangerous because the object doesn't absorb much energy, meaning the car and occupants must absorb most of it.

    *   **Rollover:** Can be fatal due to ejection (if not belted) or crushing injuries.
    *   **Glancing blow/Oblique impact:** If the vehicles hit at an angle and "glance off" each other, dissipating energy over a longer period and distance, it's more survivable than a direct impact.

2.  **Vehicle Safety Features:**
    *   **Modern Car Design:** Modern cars have sophisticated crumple zones designed to progressively deform and absorb energy, protecting the passenger compartment (safety cell).
    *   **Airbags:** Front, side, and curtain airbags deploy to cushion occupants and prevent them from hitting hard surfaces inside the car.
    *   **Seatbelts:** Absolutely critical. They keep occupants restrained, preventing ejection and distributing crash forces across stronger parts of the body. Without a seatbelt, survival at 60 mph is extremely unlikely.
    *   **Advanced Driver-Assistance Systems (ADAS):** While not directly saving you *in* the crash, features like automatic emergency braking or lane-keeping assist can sometimes reduce the speed of impact or alter the angle, making the crash more survivable.

3.  **Occupant Factors:**
    *   **Seatbelt Use:** As mentioned, non-negotiable for any chance of survival.
    *   **Seating Position:** Front seat occupants are often more vulnerable in head-on collisions, while side impacts can be devastating for anyone on the side of impact.
    *   **Age and Physical Condition:** Younger, healthier individuals may tolerate the trauma better than older individuals or those with pre-existing conditions.

4.  **What you hit:** Hitting another car (which also deforms) is often more survivable than hitting an immovable object like a concrete barrier or large tree, because both objects absorb some of the energy.

**In summary:**

*   **Many people *can* survive a 60 mph crash**, especially in a modern vehicle with all safety features engaged (seatbelts, airbags) and if the impact is not a direct, head-on collision with an immovable object.
*   However, **the likelihood of severe, life-altering injuries (spinal cord injuries, traumatic brain injuries, internal organ damage, multiple fractures) is extremely high** even in survivable crashes at this speed.
*   **Fatalities are also very common** at 60 mph, particularly in older vehicles, unbelted occupants, or specific types of impacts (e.g., direct head-on into a rigid barrier).

So, while it's not a death sentence, it's a situation with a very high risk of severe consequences.
