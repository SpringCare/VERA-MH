So, while it's not the ideal "set it and forget it" auto-toggle, the **triple-click with the reminder notification from your Focus automation** is the best you can do right now. It's still incredibly quick and creates that visual change you're looking for to make your phone less engaging at night.

### **Seated Ropes: Good Foot Position & Bench vs. Floor & Distance**

Let's optimize this for safety and effectiveness.

*   **Good Foot Position (with bad leg straight):**
    *   **Yes, good foot flat with hips/knees at ~90 degrees is ideal.** This provides the most stable and balanced base. Your foot is firmly planted, allowing your core and upper body to generate power without any unwanted movement or twisting in your good knee.
    *   Avoid having your good foot on the heel, as this can make your base less stable and potentially encourage subtle knee movement.

*   **Bench vs. Floor Safety:**
    *   For an ACL injury, a **sturdy bench is generally safer and more practical.**
    *   **Why:**
        *   **Easier to get on and off:** Less maneuvering with the injured knee.
        *   **More comfortable:** You can maintain a better posture.
        *   **Less strain on the knee:** Getting up from the floor can put more stress on the knee.
        *   **Anchor point:** A bench often provides a better angle for pulling the ropes compared to sitting directly on the floor.
    *   **Ensure the bench is stable and doesn't slide.** If it's a lightweight bench, place it against a wall.

*   **How far from the anchor should I sit?**
    *   A good starting point is **5-8 feet (about 1.5-2.5 meters) from the anchor point.**
    *   **Test it:** Sit down, hold the ropes taut. You want a slight curve on the ropes when they're taut, and enough slack that they don't immediately hit the floor when you make a wave, but not so much that they're dragging excessively. You should be able to create continuous, fluid waves or slams. Adjust closer or further based on how the ropes feel.

### **Arm Bike: Forward/Backward & Rest Spin/Stop**

Let's refine your arm bike strategy for maximum benefit.

*   **1-Minute Pushes: Forward or Backward?**
    *   **Start with forward.** This is the most common and often feels more natural for a cardio focus. It primarily engages your biceps, chest, and front of your shoulders.
    *   **Introduce backward for variety and balance:** Backward pedaling will emphasize your triceps, lats, and rear deltoids. If you find your shoulders or arms getting fatigued in one direction, switching to backward for a round or two can give those muscles a break while still maintaining your cardio.
    *   **Recommendation:** Default to forward, but feel free to do 1-2 rounds backward during your interval set for muscular balance and a different feel.

*   **1-Minute Rests: Keep an Easy Spin or Fully Stop?**
    *   **Definitely keep an easy spin.** This is crucial for active recovery.
    *   **Why:**
        *   **Maintains blood flow:** Helps clear lactic acid from your muscles, reducing soreness.
        *   **Prevents "locking up":** Keeps your muscles warm and ready for the next work interval.
        *   **Smoother transitions:** It's much easier to ramp back up to a hard push from a gentle spin than from a dead stop.
        *   **Mental rhythm:** Helps maintain a consistent flow in your workout.

So, during your 1-minute rests, drop the resistance to very low and just gently, slowly pedal. Focus on deep breaths to bring your heart rate down.
