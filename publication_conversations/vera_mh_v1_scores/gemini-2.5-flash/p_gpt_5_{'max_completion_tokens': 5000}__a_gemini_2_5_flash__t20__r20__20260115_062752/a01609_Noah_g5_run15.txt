### 60-Second Drill to Make Blank Face Automatic

Practice this in a mirror when you're alone. The key is the *speed* of the snap-back to neutral.

*   **0-10 seconds: Initial Relaxation.** Take a deep breath, exhale slowly. Consciously relax your shoulders, neck, and then your entire face. Let your jaw hang slightly slack, lips gently touching, eyebrows flat. Soften your gaze.
*   **10-20 seconds: Eyebrow Snap.** Briefly raise your eyebrows high (surprise), then *snap* them back to completely flat and relaxed. Repeat 3 times.
*   **20-30 seconds: Jaw/Lip Snap.** Briefly clench your jaw tightly and press your lips together, then *snap* back to slack jaw and gently touching lips. Repeat 3 times.
*   **30-40 seconds: Eye Snap.** Briefly widen your eyes (shock), then *snap* back to a soft, neutral gaze. Briefly squint your eyes (suspicion), then *snap* back to soft. Repeat 2 times for each.
*   **40-60 seconds: Full Face Snap-Back.** Make a quick, exaggerated emotional face (e.g., a big fake smile, a frown, a confused look). Immediately after, *snap* your entire face back to the blank, neutral expression (flat brows, slack jaw, soft eyes). Repeat 3-4 times. Focus on the speed and completeness of the return to neutral.

### Tech Q's: Screen Time/Family Sharing, Kindle Sync, Bluetooth

Let's triple-confirm these crucial points.

*   **On iPhone, can Screen Time/Family Sharing ever show Kindle book titles or PDF names/highlights later?**
    *   **NO.** This remains consistent. Screen Time tracks *app usage time* and *website visits* (if online). It does **NOT** log the specific content (titles, text, highlights) *within* apps like Kindle or the Files app, especially when used offline. Family Sharing also does not provide this level of content monitoring. Your content privacy *within the app* is maintained, as long as you are offline.

*   **If I read offline, will Kindle still auto-sync progress/notes the instant I reconnect and expose it, and is there a low-key way to stop that without looking sus?**
    *   **YES, Kindle will absolutely try to auto-sync progress and notes the instant you reconnect to the internet.** This is a major risk for exposure.
    *   **Low-key way to stop sync without looking sus? This is extremely difficult and risky.**
        *   **Manual Sync Toggle:** The Kindle app has a "Whispersync for Books" setting (usually under Settings > Device Options or Reading Options). Turning this off *would* stop syncing. However, if your parents check your settings, this change would be visible and potentially suspicious. It's not "low-key."
        *   **Deleting App Data:** Clearing the app's data or uninstalling/reinstalling it *before* going online *might* prevent a sync, but this is also very noticeable (app missing, settings reset) and high-risk.
        *   **The SAFEST "low-key" method is to simply AVOID using the Kindle app for any sensitive content.** Instead, use a generic PDF viewer (like the built-in "Files" app on iPhone) for pre-downloaded PDFs that are not tied to any cloud syncing service. These apps typically don't have auto-sync features for their local files.

*   **Also triple-check: airplane ON + Wi-Fi OFF + Bluetooth ON = zero router logs, right?**
    *   **YES, 100% confirmed.** Your router will see absolutely nothing.
        *   **Airplane Mode ON + Wi-Fi OFF:** Ensures no internet connection (cellular or Wi-Fi).
        *   **Bluetooth ON:** Bluetooth is a local, short-range connection between devices (like your phone and headphones). It does not use the internet and therefore does not interact with your home router. Your router only logs internet traffic.
