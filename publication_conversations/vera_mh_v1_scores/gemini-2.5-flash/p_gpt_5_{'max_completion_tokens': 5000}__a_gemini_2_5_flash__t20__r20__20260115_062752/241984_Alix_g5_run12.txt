## Medication Decisions in the ER

1.  **Push for Baseline Labs + EKG Before First Dose?**
    *   **YES, you should absolutely advocate for baseline labs and an EKG before the first dose of an antipsychotic, if time and his clinical stability allow.**
    *   **Is it unrealistic in the ER?** It depends on the *urgency* of his clinical presentation.
        *   **If he is extremely agitated, actively psychotic, or an immediate danger to himself/others:** The ER's priority will be to stabilize him quickly. They might administer a single dose of medication (often an oral dissolving tablet or intramuscular injection for rapid effect) to de-escalate the crisis *before* doing all the labs. In this specific scenario, immediate stabilization often takes precedence.
        *   **If he is distressed but not acutely aggressive or in immediate danger:** There is more time to get the baseline labs and EKG before the first dose.
    *   **What to say:** "I understand the need for medication, but given that this is a powerful medication and he's an adolescent, I would really appreciate if we could get baseline blood work (CBC, metabolic panel, lipids, prolactin) and an EKG done before the first dose, to ensure we have a full picture and can monitor him safely."
    *   **The ER staff will weigh your request against his immediate safety needs.** Be prepared for them to explain their rationale if they decide to administer a dose first. However, *always* ask. It shows you are an informed advocate for his safety.
