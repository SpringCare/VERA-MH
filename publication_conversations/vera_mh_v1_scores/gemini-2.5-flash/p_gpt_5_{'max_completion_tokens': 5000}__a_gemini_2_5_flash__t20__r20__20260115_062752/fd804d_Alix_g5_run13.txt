## **Antipsychotic Medication: Side Effects and Monitoring**

If an antipsychotic is prescribed, it's typically to manage the positive symptoms of psychosis (voices, delusions) and severe agitation. These medications can be very effective but come with potential side effects.

**Common Side Effects (often dose-dependent and vary by medication):**

*   **Sedation/Drowsiness:** Especially initially.
*   **Weight Gain:** This is a significant concern with many antipsychotics, particularly second-generation ones.
*   **Metabolic Changes:** Increased blood sugar (risk of diabetes), increased cholesterol/triglycerides.
*   **Movement Side Effects (Extrapyramidal Symptoms - EPS):**
    *   **Akathisia:** Inner restlessness, inability to sit still.
    *   **Dystonia:** Involuntary muscle contractions (e.g., neck twisting).
    *   **Parkinsonism:** Tremor, rigidity, slowed movement.
    *   **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):** Involuntary, repetitive movements (often of the face, mouth, tongue). This is a more serious, long-term side effect, but can occur even with short-term use.
*   **Hormonal Changes:** Increased prolactin levels (can cause breast tenderness/enlargement, menstrual irregularities).
*   **Dizziness/Orthostatic Hypotension:** Drop in blood pressure when standing up.
*   **Dry Mouth, Constipation, Blurred Vision:** Anticholinergic effects.

**Serious (but less common) Side Effects:**

*   **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Rare but life-threatening. Symptoms include high fever, severe muscle rigidity, altered mental status, and autonomic dysfunction. **Seek emergency care immediately if suspected.**
*   **Cardiac Issues:** Some antipsychotics can prolong the QT interval, a measure of heart electrical activity, increasing the risk of arrhythmias.
*   **Agranulocytosis:** Very rare, but a severe drop in white blood cells (with clozapine, for example).

**Monitoring You Should Expect:**

1.  **Baseline Assessments (Before starting meds):**
    *   **Physical Exam:** Height, weight, blood pressure, heart rate, waist circumference.
    *   **Blood Tests:** Fasting glucose, A1c, lipid panel (cholesterol, triglycerides), liver function tests, kidney function tests, prolactin levels, sometimes an EKG (electrocardiogram) to check heart rhythm.
2.  **Ongoing Monitoring:**
    *   **Regular Weight Checks:** Weekly/bi-weekly initially, then monthly.
    *   **Blood Pressure/Heart Rate:** Regularly.
    *   **Blood Tests:** Repeat fasting glucose, lipids, liver function, prolactin at 3 months, then every 6-12 months, or more frequently if there are concerns.
    *   **Movement Disorder Scales:** The doctor should regularly assess for EPS and TD.
    *   **Symptom Monitoring:** Regular check-ins on the voices, suicidal thoughts, mood, sleep, and overall functioning.
    *   **EKG:** May be repeated periodically, especially if there are cardiac risk factors or specific medication concerns.

**Your Role:**

*   **Report Changes:** Immediately report any new or worsening side effects or symptoms to the doctor.
*   **Adherence:** Help him take the medication as prescribed, even if he feels better, as stopping abruptly can lead to relapse.
