### Avoiding Stealth "Sexual Addiction/CSAT" Clinics & Record Keeping

1.  **Avoiding Stealth "Sexual Addiction/CSAT" Clinics:**
    *   **CSAT (Certified Sex Addiction Therapist) is a specific certification that some therapists obtain.** While not all CSAT therapists practice conversion therapy, the "sexual addiction" framework itself can sometimes be misused to pathologize consensual sexual behaviors or identities, or to push a "cure" for same-sex attraction.
    *   **Key Red Flags to Watch For in Profiles/Websites:**
        *   Explicit mention of "sexual addiction," "porn addiction," "unwanted same-sex attraction," "reparative therapy," "healing same-sex attraction," "sexual identity confusion" in a way that implies fixing or changing it.
        *   Heavy emphasis on "faith-based counseling," "biblical principles," or "traditional values" without clear, explicit LGBTQ+ affirmation.
        *   Lack of specific, positive LGBTQ+ affirming language (e.g., "LGBTQ+ affirming," "gay-friendly," "trans-inclusive").
    *   **Crucial Questions for the Consultation Call:**
        *   **"What is your view on sexual orientation and gender identity? Do you believe these can or should be changed through therapy?"** (The answer must be a clear NO, and an affirmation of support for authentic identity.)
        *   **"What is your approach to sex therapy, particularly if a client is exploring their sexual identity or orientation?"** (Listen for language that is affirming, non-judgmental, and client-led, not prescriptive or focused on 'curing' anything.)
        *   **"Are you a CSAT? If so, how do you integrate that training with an LGBTQ+ affirming approach?"** (This can help you gauge their perspective. Some CSATs are affirming, but you need to be very direct in asking.)

2.  **Paying Cash & Asking Not to Put a Diagnosis or SI Note in the Chart At All:**
    *   **Diagnosis:** A licensed therapist is ethically and legally required to maintain accurate clinical records, which typically includes a diagnosis if you are receiving therapy for a mental health concern. This diagnosis helps guide treatment, demonstrates medical necessity (even for self-pay), and is a standard part of their professional practice.
        *   **It's not about "flagging" you negatively.** A diagnosis (e.g., Adjustment Disorder, Major Depressive Disorder, Generalized Anxiety Disorder) is simply a way to categorize your symptoms for treatment planning.
        *   **With cash/prepaid payment, this diagnosis will *never* be shared with an insurance company, employer, or anyone else unless you explicitly consent or there's a court order.** It stays strictly within the therapist's confidential file.
    *   **SI Note ("Wish I Could Disappear"):** As discussed, expressing "I sometimes wish I could disappear" (without a plan or intent) will be noted as passive suicidal ideation (PSI). This is crucial clinical information.
        *   **A therapist cannot ethically or legally omit this from your chart.** Doing so would be considered falsifying records, which is a serious professional misconduct. It also prevents them from accurately tracking your progress, ensuring your safety, and providing the best care.
        *   **Again, this note is confidential.** It is there to help *them* help *you*, not to be used against you. When you pay cash, it further protects this information from external entities like insurance companies.
    *   **Summary:** While you can control communication and payment methods for privacy, you cannot ask a therapist to omit legally and ethically required clinical documentation like diagnoses or notes on significant distress (like PSI). Their records are for your care and their professional integrity, and they remain confidential.
