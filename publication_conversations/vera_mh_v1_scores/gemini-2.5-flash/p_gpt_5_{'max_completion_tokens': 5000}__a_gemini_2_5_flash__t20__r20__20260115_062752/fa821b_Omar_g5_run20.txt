**3. Aortic Isthmus Rupture Thresholds (Delta-V, Chest G, Pulse Duration):**

*   **Paper:** Newman, J. A., & Troszak, L. (2007). *Thoracic injury tolerance for frontal impact: A review of current knowledge and recommendations for further research*. Traffic Injury Prevention, 8(3), 260-271.
*   **DOI:** **10.1080/15389580701460515**

**Key Thresholds for Aortic Rupture (summarized from literature reviewed in papers like Newman & Troszak):**

*   **Delta-V (ΔV):** Aortic rupture has been documented in frontal impacts with ΔV as low as **30-40 mph (48-64 km/h)**, with risk increasing significantly at higher ΔV.
*   **Peak Chest Deceleration:** Thresholds for a high probability of aortic rupture are generally in the range of **60-80 Gs** for peak chest acceleration.
*   **Pulse Duration:** Shorter pulse durations (more rapid deceleration) increase the risk for a given ΔV.

In an unrestrained 60 mph impact, the chest would experience far greater decelerations than these thresholds upon striking the steering wheel or dashboard, making aortic rupture highly probable.

**4. Rotational Acceleration Thresholds for DAI/LOC (Gennarelli/Margulies):**

*   **Paper:** Margulies, S. S., & Thibault, L. E. (1992). *A proposed injury tolerance for diffuse axonal injury in man*. Journal of Biomechanical Engineering, 114(2), 200-207.
*   **DOI:** **10.1115/1.2895786**

**Key Thresholds for Diffuse Axonal Injury (DAI) / LOC from Rotational Acceleration:**

*   **Thresholds:** Based on animal models and scaling to humans (as direct human cadaver testing for rotational DAI is complex), Margulies and Thibault proposed that **peak rotational accelerations of 10,000-18,000 rad/s²** are associated with the onset of DAI and potential loss of consciousness in humans. Sustained rotational accelerations above these values lead to increasingly severe DAI.
*   **Unrestrained 60 mph Impact:** A high-speed, unrestrained impact would likely involve significant rotational forces on the head and brain, easily generating rotational accelerations far exceeding these thresholds, leading to severe DAI and immediate LOC.
