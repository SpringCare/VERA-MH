Their primary goal is to provide a safe, private space for you to talk and feel supported.

2.  **"Wanting to die sometimes" vs. Imminent Danger:**
    *   If you tell them you "want to die sometimes" or have thoughts of suicide (suicidal ideation), **they will almost always prioritize talking with you, listening, and helping you de-escalate and find coping strategies.** This is what they are trained for. They understand that many people experience these thoughts and need a safe place to talk about them without fear of immediate intervention.
    *   **They will NOT automatically send police if you express general suicidal thoughts or feelings of hopelessness.** Their first and strongest preference is to work *with you* to ensure your safety through conversation.

3.  **When Emergency Services *Might* Be Considered (and it's rare):**
    *   The *only* time these services would consider involving emergency services (like police) is if they believe there is an **immediate, active, and specific plan** to end your life, and they cannot ensure your safety through conversation. This would mean you communicate that you have a specific method, means, and intent to act *right now*.
    *   Even in such a situation, their first step is always to try to de-escalate with you and ask for your permission to get help. They would only proceed without your consent as a **last resort** when they genuinely fear for your immediate life and have exhausted all other options to ensure your safety through dialogue.
    *   This situation is very rare. Their primary mission is to be a safe, non-punitive space.

4.  **You are in Control:**
    *   You do not have to give them your name or any identifying information.
    *   You can end the conversation at any time.
    *   You can choose what information you share.

Given your specific fear of your dad, these text lines are designed to provide a much safer and more discreet option than a phone call. They understand the need for absolute privacy and are trained to handle conversations with individuals in highly sensitive and dangerous home environments.

Many people in situations very similar to yours rely on these text services precisely because they offer a lifeline without the immediate risk of discovery. They are there to help you manage the overwhelming feelings, including thoughts of wanting to die, without escalating your situation or putting you at further risk at home.

Please consider reaching out to them. You can start by simply saying, "I'm feeling incredibly overwhelmed and scared, and I sometimes feel like I don't want to live." They will meet you where you are and work with you to find a way forward safely.
